## [2.2.0] — Unreleased

### Overview

Version 2.2.0 is primarily a **performance release**. The headline gains come from two largely independent strands that compound where they overlap:

1. **Mathematical: a new analytical decomposition for the underlying sums.**

   Three core quantities underlie the closed-form expectation-tensor calculus: the **inner product** $\langle T_x, T_y \rangle = \int T_x T_y \, \mathrm{d}z$ between two tensors (which underlies cosine similarity, windowed similarity, and Rényi-2 entropy); **point evaluation** $T(q)$ of a single tensor at a query (a sum of Gaussian-kernel contributions, one per source $r$-tuple — no integration involved at the level of the quantity, since $T$ is already the density. Note that the *Möbius realisation* of point evaluation and total mass is nonetheless grid-free only in absolute mode; in relative mode it integrates the translation marginal on a u-grid — see the relative-mode note below); and **total mass** $Z = \int T \, \mathrm{d}z$ of a single tensor (which converts $T$ to a probability density via $T/Z$ and appears in the denominator of Rényi-2 entropy and other normalised quantities). All three reduce to sums over slot-tuples: a double sum (one $r$-tuple per side) for the inner product, a single sum for the other two. v2.0 / v2.1 already evaluated all three analytically — for the inner product via *Bulger's method*, for the others via an explicit centres-array route. v2.2 adds a new decomposition of these sums, **the Möbius method**, that scales far better at high $r$ and high $K$ and uniformly covers all three.

   **Bulger's method** (v1, carried unchanged into v2.0 / v2.1) is specific to the inner product. It organises the slot-tuple sum by taking combinations on one side and permutations on the other, exploiting the within-tuple multinomial symmetry to avoid enumerating equivalent orderings. The asymmetric combinations-vs-permutations organisation requires two sides to be present, so the method does not extend to point evaluation or total mass.

   **The Möbius method**, new in v2.2, is a different decomposition that applies uniformly to all three quantities. The slot-tuple sum is rewritten via Möbius inversion on the partition lattice: the distinct-index constraint becomes an alternating sum over set partitions of the slot indices, with each partition's term factorising across its blocks. For the **inner product** specifically, the partition decomposition combines with **orbit collapse** under the joint slot-permutation symmetry — many partition-pairs are redundant in the sense of contributing identically to the sum, so each redundant family is summed once with a counted multiplicity rather than walked term-by-term. This collapses the $B_r^2$ partition-pair count down to $|\Omega_r|$ orbit equivalence classes (4, 10, 33, 92, 306, 948, 3210 for $r = 2, \ldots, 8$, versus $B_r^2$ as large as $\sim 1.7 \times 10^7$ at $r = 8$). For **point evaluation** and **total mass** there is only one side, so no joint symmetry to exploit and no collapse to perform; cost scales with the bare partition count $|\Pi_r| = B_r$.

   The underlying inclusion-exclusion technique itself has a longer history in MPT: it was first published in Milne et al. 2011 — framed there as inclusion-exclusion rather than as Möbius inversion on the partition lattice (the two are equivalent formulations) — and has been used in the toolbox since v1 for *numerical* computation of discrete tensors. What is new in v2.2 is its application as an analytical decomposition of the slot-tuple sum, its combination with orbit collapse for the inner product, and its uniform extension across all three core quantities. The decomposition is grid-free in **absolute** mode (each partition block collapses to an $O(K)$ event sum evaluated directly at the query). In **relative** mode it is *not* grid-free: the relative tensor is the marginal of the absolute tensor over the shared translation $u$, and the alternating partition sum only factorises across slots at fixed $u$, so the Möbius method integrates over a u-grid of $N_u$ points (as the centres/Bulger path avoids by carrying the closed-form relative Gaussian per tuple instead). Removing that $u$-integral analytically would re-expand the per-partition product of block sums back into an $O(K^r)$ tuple enumeration, which is exactly what the decomposition exists to avoid; the u-grid trades that enumeration for an $N_u$ factor. The u-grid is therefore intrinsic to the relative-mode realisation — but the $K$ factor at each grid node is not: in the non-periodic case each partition block's integrand factor separates exactly into a variance prefactor times a shifted read-back of one of $r$ precomputed smoothed event distributions $S_m$ (one per block size, width $\sigma/\sqrt{m}$), so `evalOrbitRel` carries three strategies. The *direct* strategy costs $B_r \cdot r \cdot K \cdot N_u$ per query. The *factored* strategy costs $B_r \cdot r \cdot N_u$ per query after an amortised $O(\sum_m N_{\text{fine},m} \cdot K)$ tabulation. The *spectral* strategy removes the u-grid entirely: the translation integral constrains each partition's total mode index to zero, so every partition is a coefficient of a product of per-block Fourier spectra, and the spectra of a Gaussian mixture are closed form. Its cost is $O(K \cdot M)$ for the coefficients plus a per-query cost in $M$ alone, where $M \propto L / \sigma$ is the mode count over the period $L$ (the true period in periodic mode; a padded embedding period on the line) and $K$ has left the per-query term altogether. A per-call cost gate selects among the three. The factored read-back (local quintic interpolation, identical stencil in both languages) targets a relative accuracy at the kernel-truncation floor $\exp(-k^2/2)$ set by `truncation_sigmas` (clamped to $[10^{-12}, 10^{-3}]$; the default $k = \infty$ targets $10^{-12}$, the noise level of the u-grid quadrature itself). In periodic relative mode the factored and spectral strategies apply where the per-block variance/mean reduction survives wrapping — the truncation window and each query's position span must both fit within half the circle — and the direct strategy covers the rest. All three figures compare with the grid-free $B_r \cdot r \cdot K$ of absolute mode.

   **How the Möbius and Bulger methods relate.** For the **inner product** they are alternative decompositions of the same analytical integral. On any given inner-product call the dispatcher picks one and computes the value via that decomposition; the two are not combined within a single call. This exclusivity is pragmatic, not mathematical — both methods already exhaust the same underlying slot-permutation symmetry, just along different routes, so there is no natural way to combine them that would gain over picking the better of the two. Each has a different cost-scaling profile: Bulger's method has essentially no per-call fixed overhead beyond the slot-tuple loop; the Möbius method carries fixed per-call overhead (orbit-table lookup, $|\Omega_r|$ tensor contractions) that must be amortised over the savings from orbit collapse. Bulger's method therefore tends to win at small $r$ and small $K$ (short slot-tuple loops, Möbius's overhead doesn't pay off); the Möbius method wins decisively at large $r$ or large $K$. The cost-model dispatcher in strand 2 picks the cheaper of the two per call. For **point evaluation** and **total mass** Bulger's method does not apply, so there is no two-decomposition choice; v2.2's Möbius method here is an *efficiency alternative* to v2.1's centres-array route — same analytical value, computed without materialising the explicit $r$-tuple centres array, which matters most in the large-$K$ / high-$r$ regime where that array would dominate memory.

   The strand also includes the **ragged-K hybrid** for variable-cardinality MA inner-product inputs (a per-event safe/unsafe partition with an exact direct-enumeration fallback for unsafe events).

2. **Computational: dispatching, kernel precision, kernel truncation, and batching refinements.** A per-call cost-model dispatcher (with a timing-probe fallback for indeterminate SA cosine-similarity regimes) selects whichever decomposition is faster for each call. Two new kernel-evaluation controls — `truncation_sigmas` (skip Gaussian contributions beyond $k$ standard deviations from a centre) and `kernel_precision` (`'single'` instead of `'double'` for the kernel matrix multiplication; ~2× speedup, ~7 sig fig precision) — apply to the centres-path consumers (eval, cos-sim, entropy) at user request and roughly compound with the mathematical-strand gains. Set per call or globally via the new toolbox-wide defaults API (`mpt.set_default` / `mptDefaults`). **Lazy density-struct construction** in `buildExpTens` is also part of this strand: expensive density fields (`U_perm`, `wJ`, `V_comb`, `wV_comb`) are deferred until a consumer needs them — most strand-1 consumers now don't. Finally, **several batching refinements** replace v2.1 sequential per-item loops with grouped contractions: K-grouped direct enumeration for unsafe-event pairs in the MA inner product, a dedup-and-batch rewrite of `tensorHarmonicity`'s batched mode, and, in the Möbius relative-mode evaluator, vectorised u-grid handling plus a factored integrand strategy that removes the per-node $K$ factor for batched workloads (accuracy tied to `truncation_sigmas`; see the relative-mode note above and the evaluator bullet below). Each is detailed in the corresponding section below.

v2.2 also exposes **Rényi-2 differential entropy** $H_2 = -\log_b \langle T, T\rangle / Z^2$ as `method='renyi2'` on `entropyExpTens`. The analytical route was conceptually available in v2.0 / v2.1 (it requires only the inner product and total mass, both already analytical); v2.2 wires it up as a user-facing option and gains efficiency at high $r$ / $K$ via the Möbius method. **Shannon differential entropy** has no closed form in any version and is unchanged — `method='shannon'` continues to use a numerical grid.

Additional improvements outside the two strands: **`bindEvents`** accepts $K_{a, n} > 1$ input, unblocking polyphonic-binding analyses with multi-slot attributes at the source. A new pre-tensor preprocessing primitive **`translateAttributes` / `translate_attributes`** shifts selected attributes' values by a chosen offset (unwrapped on periodic groups, with the periodic kernel handling wrap downstream; structural no-op on relative groups); this is the pre-tensor route to sliding-comparison along an attribute axis, complementary to the post-tensor `windowedSimilarity`, and handles multi-slot attributes ($K_{a, n} > 1$) without ambiguity since every slot translates by the same offset. Where that sliding comparison is a *sweep* — one context density against many uniform translates of one query — **`sweepCosSimExpTens` / `sweep_cos_sim_exp_tens`** evaluates the whole sweep in one pass rather than one inner product per offset, by separating each tuple pair's contribution into a fixed shape weight and a Gaussian in the offset.

The release is additive in its API: existing v2.1.0 calling conventions are preserved, and the method dispatcher (`'auto'`) reproduces v2.1's decomposition choice at the floating-point level in standard regimes. The new method choice is exposed via a `method` keyword on `cosSimExpTens`, `evalExpTens`, and `entropyExpTens` (accepting `'auto'`, `'bulger'`, `'mobius'`, plus `'centres'` for eval and `'direct'` for small problems); `'auto'` is the default. `kernel_precision` defaults to `'double'`. **`truncation_sigmas` now defaults to `6`** (see *Changed* below): default-configured output is therefore a ~6-significant-figure approximation of the untruncated v2.1 result rather than bit-identical to it, and exact v2.1 numerics are recoverable at any time with `truncation_sigmas=inf`.

**Terminology used throughout.** *SA* = single-attribute (one density coordinate per event, e.g. pitch alone); *MA* = multi-attribute (multiple coordinate axes per event, e.g. pitch + onset time). A *multi-attribute expectation tensor* (MAET) is a density built from MA inputs. *Tensor order* $r$ (or $r_a$ for attribute $a$) is the number of slots in each event's ordered-tuple expansion (e.g. $r = 3$ for triadic interval analysis). For each event $n$ and attribute $a$ the *effective slot count* $K_{\text{eff}, n, a}$ is the number of non-NaN entries; the *attribute width* $K_a$ is the constant maximum over events. *Ragged-K* inputs are those where $K_{\text{eff}, n, a}$ varies across events $n$ (e.g. a sequence mixing dyads, triads, and tetrads), so trailing NaN entries pad each event to the common width $K_a$. *Safe* on attribute $a$ for event $n$ means $K_{\text{eff}, n, a} - r_a \ge 2$ (sufficient slot margin for the Möbius method's alternating partition sum — the inclusion-exclusion sum over set partitions of an $r$-tuple, with alternating signs derived from the Möbius function on the partition lattice — to retain precision); *unsafe* means $K_{\text{eff}, n, a} - r_a < 2$, where the alternating-sum cancellation would corrupt the result and a direct ordered-tuple enumeration is used instead. The *Bell number* $B_r$ is the count of set partitions of an $r$-element set ($B_2 = 2$, $B_3 = 5$, ..., $B_7 = 877$, $B_8 = 4140$); orbit-table size scales with $B_r$ and dominates the build cost beyond $r = 8$. *Inner product* (abbreviated *IP* throughout) refers to $\langle T_x, T_y \rangle$. *Canonical-form deduplication* refers to the batched-input optimisation introduced in v2.1: rows that are symmetry-equivalent (e.g. permutations of the same slot multiset, transpositions in relative mode) are reduced to a canonical key, computed once, and the result mapped back to all matching rows. *ULP* = "unit in the last place", the smallest representable spacing in floating-point — a few-ULP difference between two computations is the order of bit-level noise from accumulation reordering and is invisible above three or four significant figures.

### Added — Möbius method (and Bulger's method retained)

- **`method` keyword on `cosSimExpTens`, `evalExpTens`, `entropyExpTens`.** Accepts `'auto'` (default), `'bulger'` (the v2.0 / v2.1 inner-product decomposition; IP-only), `'mobius'` (the new partition-decomposition method, with orbit collapse applied in the IP case), `'centres'` (eval only, the explicit centres-array path), and `'direct'` (small problems where direct enumeration beats both). The `'auto'` setting routes via a per-call cost model that considers $r$, per-attribute $K_a$, event counts, $\sigma/\text{period}$ for periodic-relative groups, and (for MA) the per-attribute safe/unsafe partition. For SA cosine similarity, the dispatcher additionally uses a **timing probe** when the cost-model pre-screen is not decisive (i.e., when neither method's estimated cost dominates the other by ≥10× at the call's parameters): on a small subset of the workload the dispatcher times each candidate method and routes the full call accordingly. The probe-based path is future-proof against incremental optimisations on either side (gains automatically reflected) and replaces the older purely-analytical heuristic for the cos-sim path; the eval dispatcher retains the analytical form since the centres path is cheap enough that probing adds more overhead than it saves. Backward compatibility: at default settings on standard inputs the dispatcher's chosen method produces v2.1.0-equivalent results to floating-point tolerance (a few ULP differences are possible where the probe selects Möbius and the v2.1 default was Bulger, since the two are mathematically equivalent but accumulate in different orders).

- **Pre-built orbit tables for $r \in \{2, \ldots, 8\}$.** Möbius coefficients, partition catalogues, and contraction edge lists are pre-built and shipped in both languages (Python `.pkl`, MATLAB `.mat`); at runtime the Möbius method looks up the table for the given $r$ and contracts, with no enumeration cost paid per call. The shipped maximum is exposed as `_ORBIT_R_MAX_SHIPPED = 8` (Python) and the matching `% _ORBIT_R_MAX_SHIPPED` boundary in MATLAB; the cost-model dispatcher consults it to route away from Möbius at $r > 8$ so users don't pay an unexpected on-demand build cost on first call. Wheel/sdist builds carry the seven shipped pickles via the new `[tool.setuptools.package-data]` block in `pyproject.toml`. Bell-number growth makes higher-$r$ tables expensive: $r = 8$ is 1.65 MB and roughly 3 minutes to build; $r = 9$ would be ~3 MB and ~80 minutes. Users wanting Möbius at $r > 8$ opt in by accepting the build cost on first call (subsequently cached on disk under `~/.mpt/orbit_tables/` or `MPT_CACHE_DIR`); a cost-preview helper prints estimated build time and Bell-number scaling context before the build begins, suppressible via `MPT_NO_BUILD_WARN=1`. A hard cap at $r = 12$ remains for the build path.

- **`cancellationThreshold` keyword on `cosSimExpTens`.** Default `1e-12`. Guards the Möbius method's alternating partition sum against catastrophic cancellation: if the worst-case cancellation ratio across orbit terms drops below the threshold, the dispatcher falls back to Bulger's method. The same threshold gates the Möbius path's three-layer guard for SA cosine similarity (cross-cancellation, corruption, severe ratio).

- **`+mobius` package (MATLAB) / `_mobius.py` module (Python).** Houses the Möbius-method implementation: `innerProductOrbit`, `innerProductOrbitPwBatched`, `innerProductOrbitGrid`, `evalOrbitAbs`, `evalOrbitRel`, `totalMassAbs`, `totalMassRel`, `getOrbitTable`, `buildOrbitTable`, `canonicalForm`, `contract`, and supporting partition / set-partition / contingency-table enumerators. All public-facing operations on densities continue to dispatch through the high-level wrappers (`cosSimExpTens`, `evalExpTens`, etc.); the module is internal but documented for users wanting to understand or extend the machinery. (The legacy `'orbit'` naming inside this module reflects its origin and is retained as an internal label; the user-facing API uses `method='mobius'`.)

### Added — Memoised self inner products, the `'oneSidedDenom'` $\langle X, X\rangle$ skip, and a direct all-$r = 1$ inner-product route

- **Self inner products are memoised on the density.** A cosine-similarity triple needs three inner products — the cross term $\langle X, Y\rangle$ and the two self terms $\langle X, X\rangle$, $\langle Y, Y\rangle$ — and the self terms depend only on their own density, whose contents never change after construction. Each density now stores its self inner product on first computation and reuses it thereafter, keyed by everything the value depends on beyond the density itself: the decomposition route (the Bulger and Möbius conventions carry different constant prefactors that cancel only within a single route's triple), the resolved `truncationSigmas`, the kernel precision, and — on the Möbius route — the per-attribute closed-form-vs-grid choices, which alter the per-attribute prefactor. A sweep of many query densities against one context (`cosSimExpTens(densX, {d1, ..., dM})`, or the raw scalar-vs-list form) therefore pays the context's $O(N^2)$ self term once rather than $M$ times; the marginal cost per query is the $O(Nm)$ cross term plus the query's own small self term. In Python, `pruned` is memoised for the same reason: the similarity path prunes both operands per comparison, and returning one shared pruned object is what lets the memoised self term persist across a sweep. The dispatcher purges Möbius-route entries whenever its post-hoc impossible-value guard trips, so a rejected run never seeds the memo.

  The two languages realise the memo through their native semantics. Python attaches it to the (reference-semantics) density object, so it persists across separate calls automatically. MATLAB density structs are value types, so the memo there is carried by value: within one `cosSimExpTens` call the sweep loops thread it across pairs — which covers the sweep forms with no calling-convention change — and for a caller's own loop of scalar calls the density-struct scalar form offers optional cache-carrying outputs, `[s, densX, densY] = cosSimExpTens(densX, densY, ...)`, attaching the memo to the returned structs (a `selfIP` field) so the caller can thread the reference density through the loop. The extra outputs are refused with a clear error outside that form, where the internal threading already applies. The similarity values are identical in both languages and under either usage; the languages differ only in whether memoisation crosses call boundaries unaided.

- **`normalize='oneSidedDenom'` no longer computes $\langle X, X\rangle$.** That normalisation's denominator is the second operand's self term alone, so the first operand's self term is not consumed; it is now computed only when the `'cosine'` normalisation (or a memo hit, which is free) supplies a reason to have it. For the sweep shape above this removes the dominant term outright: at a 1,200-event context the per-offset cost of the benchmark configuration falls from ~42 ms to under 1 ms, and scaling in the context size returns to the linear behaviour the computed quantity implies.

- **Direct route for the all-$r = 1$ shape.** When every attribute has $r_a = 1$, each event contributes a single joint kernel and the inner product is a smoothed cross-correlation — the simplest shape the framework supports. A dedicated routine now computes it with a single accumulator, in-place arithmetic, and a plain exponential, in place of the generic multi-attribute path's per-attribute difference tensors and masked log-space exponential. The quantity computed is identical — same per-attribute terms in the same accumulation order, same truncation threshold including the summed-terms tightening, same chunking heuristic under `kernel_chunk_bytes` — and validation reproduces the generic path to the last bit across the absolute, periodic, and mixed mode combinations (relative attributes at $r_a = 1$ have a vanishing quadratic form and contribute nothing, exactly as the generic path evaluates them).

- **Point-set-shaped builds and sweeps are further specialised.** Two additions target the shape where each event carries one value per attribute at $r_a = 1$ (one note per event — the configuration a MAET offers in place of point-set query matching). First, the per-tuple field assembly recognises that shape directly: the perm- and comb-side arrays are the input value matrices themselves, the per-tuple weight is the product of the event's per-attribute weights, and the tuple index is the event index, so the general per-event, per-attribute enumeration machinery — which dominated the build cost of such densities — is bypassed, with field-for-field identical output (NaN-padded events fall through to the general path and its diagnostics). Second, a broadcast of one shared density against many queries of identical all-$r = 1$ geometry evaluates every cross inner product in a single batched kernel pass over the concatenated query columns, amortising the per-pair dispatch that otherwise dominates such sweeps; the truncation threshold is applied per column segment with each pair's own term count, so the set of zeroed kernel entries matches the per-pair path exactly, and self terms flow through the same memoisation keys, so subsequent per-pair calls see the same cache state. Any structural mismatch in the list bypasses the batched path and surfaces through the ordinary per-pair route with its usual diagnostics. Values agree with per-pair evaluation to the toolbox parity floor (the final contraction associates differently, a machine-epsilon effect); chunk width is additionally capped to a cache-resident block, since a single monolithic kernel block at large event counts leaves cache and forfeits the batched path's advantage. On the benchmark sweep configuration (1,200-event context, 3-event query, `'oneSidedDenom'`), repeated-minimum timing measures ~0.16 ms per offset in Python against ~42 ms before this release's sweep work — roughly 270×, and within a small factor of a hand-written direct evaluation of the same quantity.

- **Fixed: the dispatcher's memory-safety early return ignored `return_costs`.** The Bulger-vs-Möbius selector's working-set guard returned a bare route string where every other early return respects the `return_costs` calling convention, so any caller requesting predicted costs (the dispatch explainer, calibration tooling) raised an unpacking error whenever the guard fired at large working sets. The guard now returns the `(route, NaN, NaN)` triple, uniformly with the other structural decisions.

- **Cost models follow the computed work.** The pre-computation time estimate counts only the inner products the call will actually evaluate — memoised self terms and a skipped $\langle X, X\rangle$ are excluded — and the Bulger-vs-Möbius dispatcher's two pricing formulae accept per-route flags that exclude self matrices the call will not compute, so near-crossover routing reflects the marginal cost of each route rather than its full-triple cost. Default pricing with no flags set is unchanged.

### Added — Translation sweeps as a weighted Gaussian mixture in the offset

- **`sweepCosSimExpTens` / `sweep_cos_sim_exp_tens`.** Comparing one density against uniformly translated copies of another previously cost one inner product per offset. It now costs one pass over the tuple pairs plus $M$ evaluations of a mixture in the offset. The identity: on an absolute attribute the quadratic form separates as $Q_a(d - \mu_a \mathbf{1}) = r_a (\mu_a - \bar{d})^2 + \sum_i (d_i - \bar{d})^2$, where $\bar{d}$ is the mean of the tuple-pair difference. The second term does not depend on the offset, so each (tuple pair, attribute) contributes a fixed *shape* weight and a *placement* Gaussian in the offset, centred at $\bar{d}$ with effective width $\sigma_a / \sqrt{r_a}$. Placement and shape separate exactly. The integral of the profile recovers the relative-mode inner product, $\int \langle T_X, T_{Y + \mu} \rangle \, \mathrm{d}\mu = \langle T_X, T_Y \rangle_{\mathrm{rel}} \sqrt{4 \pi \sigma_a^2 / r_a}$, which is the same decomposition read in the other direction.

  Two properties make the reduction exact rather than approximate. The shape term is the *relative*-mode quadratic form, so it is computed as a Gram matrix of within-tuple-centred coordinates and no $r_a \times n_J \times n_K$ difference array is ever formed. And the truncation rule is the one the per-offset path applies, a floor on the accumulated log-kernel: because the placement term is non-positive, a component whose shape weight alone falls below the floor cannot rise above it at any offset, so dropping it before the sweep begins changes nothing. Both self inner products are invariant under a uniform translation of their own values, so each is computed once for the whole sweep rather than once per offset.

  Measured on a motif-in-passage sweep (400-event context, 8-event query, 360 offsets, two attributes, self terms warm): 36× at $K_{a,n} = r_a = 1$, 143× at $K_{a,n} = r_a = 3$, and 412× at $K_{a,n} = r_a = 4$ on a 120-event context. The saving is bounded by the offset count and erodes as the one-off mixture build comes to dominate, which is what the identity implies.

- **A second route for high tuple order, selected by `method`.** `method='mixture'` is the placement/shape split above; `method='orbit'` instead evaluates the Möbius/orbit inner product at the shifted values, with the (event pair, offset) index riding the orbit routine's batch axis, so its cost scales with $|\Omega_r|$ rather than with the tuple-pair count $[C(K_a, r_a) \, r_a!]^2$ that the mixture must both enumerate and store. `method='auto'` (the default) compares the two costs and picks. Measured on a single-attribute sweep at 180 offsets, the mixture leads by 22× at $(K_{a,n}, r_a) = (6, 4)$ and the orbit route by 16× at $(10, 4)$ and 33× at $(10, 5)$, with the crossover near 3 × 10⁶ tuple pairs. The two constants governing the choice are machine-specific in the same way as `orbitCostIntercept`; **`matlab/tools/calibrateSweepRoute.m`** measures them on a given machine and prints the values to use. Correctness does not depend on them, since either route computes the same quantity.

- **Attribute modes.** An attribute is handled by the split when it is flat or nested, non-periodic, and isotropic. Absolute contributes both a shape and a placement term; relative contributes the shape term alone, since the relative quadratic form *is* the shape term — a uniform translation cancels in every within-tuple difference, which is the same statement as having no placement term, and is why a relative attribute cannot be swept. A nested attribute resolved to an inner or intermediate co-transposition unit is that same case read per block: its form is the sum over blocks of each block's relative form, so it contributes one shape term per block, no placement term, and cannot be swept either. A periodic attribute that is never translated contributes an offset-independent factor through the same wrapped kernel the per-offset path applies; a *swept* periodic attribute is carried by the orbit route, which never forms the split and so is indifferent to periodicity. The orbit route is symmetric-only — the decomposition sums over unordered value subsets with multiplicity — so it declines an ordered attribute rather than silently symmetrising, and `'auto'` falls back to the mixture there.

- **Relative-and-periodic attributes** are never swept (a uniform translation is a no-op there) but may contribute their fixed factor. That factor is one of two measures — the single-wrap and the transposition-average kernel — which coincide only below a $\sigma / P$ limit, so the limit the inner-product dispatcher already calibrates governs acceptance: below it the two agree inside the floor `truncation_sigmas` implies, and no choice is being made silently. Above it the attribute's `wrap` decides, exactly as the per-offset dispatcher resolves the same question: `'single-image'` names the measure the mixture computes, `'full-image'` (the default) the one the orbit route computes.

- **Reaching the reduction without changing the call site.** A sweep produced by `translate_attributes` carries the offsets that generated it, and `cos_sim_exp_tens` reads them and routes accordingly, so existing Python pipelines gain the speed-up unchanged; the carrier is a `list` subclass (`TranslatedSweep`), so every existing consumer still sees a list. MATLAB cell arrays cannot carry attached data, so `translateAttributes` returns the same information as a fourth output, `sweep`, for the caller to pass on. The offsets are *carried*, never recovered from the translated values: recovering them would mean comparing floating-point differences against a tolerance, and no tolerance both admits every honestly translated sweep — where $(p + \mu) - p$ differs from $\mu$ in the last bits by an amount varying with each value's magnitude — and preserves the parity floor. A sweep assembled by hand therefore takes the per-offset path and is correct there.

### Added — Non-periodic quadratic forms as Gram matrices

- In every non-periodic mode the quadratic form is a squared Euclidean distance between (possibly quotiented) coordinates, so the shared multi-attribute log-kernel now computes it as a Gram matrix, $\lVert u \rVert^2 + \lVert v \rVert^2 - 2 u \cdot v$, in place of an $(r_a, n_J, n_K)$ difference array: absolute on the raw coordinates, relative on tuple-centred ones, nested on block-centred ones. Periodic attributes keep the difference path, where the wrap makes the form non-Euclidean. Measured on the log-kernel alone: 2.1× at $r = 3$ absolute, 2.7× at $r = 3$ relative, and 3.4× at $r = 4$ relative with two attributes. Values are bit-identical. The gain reaches multi-attribute work, since single-attribute inner products take the spatial-index helper rather than this routine.

- Both operands are shifted by one of the attribute's own values before the Gram matrix is formed. The identity cancels two large numbers when the coordinates sit far from the origin, which costs significant digits — measured against the difference form, the log-kernel departs by $4 \times 10^{-3}$ at coordinate magnitude $10^6$ and $5 \times 10^{-1}$ at $10^7$. Every quantity here depends on the operands only through their differences, so a shift shared by both is exact; taking it from the data rather than from a mean makes the subtraction itself exact as well (Sterbenz), where a mean would inject a rounding error at just those magnitudes. A guard compares the residual rounding, $\varepsilon s^2 / (4\sigma_a^2)$ for spread $s$, against the floor `truncation_sigmas` implies, and takes the difference form where the Gram form would not meet it — so a caller asking for accuracy-floor accuracy at small $\sigma_a$ gets the exact route and one asking for the default gets the fast one.

### Fixed — The per-attribute wrap survives density rebuilds

- Two functions that rebuild a density dropped its per-attribute `wrap`, which defaults to `'full-image'`: MATLAB's `internal.ensureExpTensExpensive`, which materialises the deferred fields of a lazily built density, and Python's `MaetDensity._pruned_compute`, which drops zero-weight events. A density built with `'single-image'` therefore reverted silently — changing the *measure* rather than the speed. Because `buildExpTens` defaults to lazy construction and `cosSimExpTens`'s pairwise route materialises, an ordinary MATLAB `cosSimExpTens` call on a `'single-image'` density computed the full-image measure. The defect is invisible below the $\sigma / P$ threshold, where the two measures agree inside the accuracy floor, which is why it went unnoticed. Each language happened to get the other function right: MATLAB's `internal.prunedExpTens` carried the field, and Python's lazy materialisation shares the object.

### Added — Kernel-evaluation controls (truncation and precision)

These controls govern how the dense Gaussian-kernel matrix is computed in the "centres path" — the path used by `evalExpTens`, `cosSimExpTens`, and `entropyExpTens` whenever the dispatcher selects the explicit-centres-array evaluation strategy (every $r$-tuple in the source cartesian product is materialised as a Gaussian centre, and each query point is evaluated by summing kernel contributions from all centres). The Möbius method uses neither control: it evaluates the same density analytically without forming the centres matrix.

- **`truncation_sigmas` (Python) / `truncationSigmas` (MATLAB).** Type `float`, default `6`. At the default $k = 6$, the Gaussian kernel contribution from each centre $\mathbf{c}$ to each query $\mathbf{x}$ is skipped whenever the Mahalanobis distance $\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}\|_Q$ exceeds $k \sigma$, equivalently whenever the kernel value drops below the floor $\exp(-k^2 / 2)$. ($Q$ is the kernel's inverse-covariance metric; for an isotropic Gaussian $Q = I/\sigma^2$ and the test reduces to $\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}\|_2 > k\sigma$.) The skip is implemented as a grid-bucket spatial index that avoids forming the dense kernel matrix for the discarded entries, saving the dominant `exp` and matrix-multiply cost on values that would round to negligible contributions anyway. Truncation perturbs the result by a small absolute amount bounded by the $k\sigma$ tail $\exp(-k^2/2)$: the worst-case error versus the exact result is about 1e-3 at $k = 4$, 1e-5 at $k = 5$, 2e-8 at the default $k = 6$, and 1e-10 at $k = 7$, reaching the cross-language 1e-12 parity floor by $k = 8$. This is an absolute error — on a cosine similarity directly, and on an evaluated density as a fraction of its scale — and is somewhat larger at high tensor order $r$, where more pairwise coordinates can sit near the truncation boundary at once; the relative error at query points deep in the tails is unbounded but falls on negligible-mass points that are never summed, integrated, or compared. Set `truncation_sigmas` / `truncationSigmas` to `math.inf` / `Inf` for the exact, untruncated result (the reference against which the toolbox's cross-language 1e-12 parity is verified); each added sigma shrinks the error by $\exp(-(2k+1)/2)$ at a cost that grows as $k^{r}$ with tensor order. Affects all centres-path consumers (eval, cos-sim, entropy) in both SA and MA modes.

- **`kernel_precision` (Python) / `kernelPrecision` (MATLAB).** Accepts `'double'` (default) or `'single'`, the latter casting the kernel-matrix arithmetic to `float32` for a ~2× speedup at the cost of approximately 7 significant figures of precision (vs approximately 15 for double). The cast applies only to the kernel matrix and its immediate matrix multiplication; density coordinates and the final summation in cosine / entropy are preserved at full double precision. Recommended for large-$K$ eval and centres-path cos-sim at scale, where the user is interested in similarities to three or four significant figures and the speed-up is substantial.

- **Toolbox-wide defaults.** A new defaults module exposes `mpt.set_default`, `mpt.get_default`, `mpt.get_defaults`, and `mpt.reset_defaults` in Python; MATLAB exposes the same functionality via `mptDefaults`: `mptDefaults` (no arguments) returns a struct of current values, `mptDefaults('name')` returns one value, `mptDefaults('name', value, ...)` sets one or more values and returns the previous values for save-and-restore idioms, `mptDefaults('reset')` returns to factory values. Per-call kwargs always override defaults. Defaults persist within a single Python process (not across processes) and within a MATLAB session (not across `clear all`). Factory defaults are `truncation_sigmas` / `truncationSigmas` $=$ `6`, `kernel_precision` / `kernelPrecision` $=$ `'double'`, and `show_hints` / `showHints` $=$ `True` (gates the toolbox's informational dispatch-decision messages; set to `false` to silence). The untruncated-double bypass shortcuts out the grid-bucket path when `truncation_sigmas` is `Inf` and `kernel_precision` is `'double'`, so a caller who opts back into the exact regime sees byte-exact untruncated numerics with no truncation-index overhead.

### Added — Rényi-2 differential entropy

- **`method='renyi2'` on `entropyExpTens` / `entropy_exp_tens`.** Computes $H_2 = -\log_b(\langle T, T\rangle / Z^2)$ in closed form via the Möbius method's inner product for $\langle T, T\rangle$ and the Möbius method's total mass for $Z$. SA paths use `mobius.orbitInnerAbsSA` / `mobius.orbitInnerRelSA` and `mobius.totalMassAbs` / `mobius.totalMassRel`. MA paths factorise $\langle T, T\rangle = \sum_n \prod_a I_a^{(n,n)}$ via the per-attribute IP matrix and likewise use a per-event total-mass product for $Z$. The default remains `method='shannon'` (numerical Shannon entropy on a discretised grid), so existing callers see no behavioural change. `r=1` rel returns 0 by convention (0-D relative space; constant density), matching the spec; `r=1` abs uses a direct kernel-sum branch since the orbit table is undefined at $r=1$. `normalize=True` with `method='renyi2'` raises `NotImplementedError` — the natural normaliser $\log_b(V)$ yields a $(-\infty, 1]$ range rather than Shannon's $[0, 1]$, so a uniform normaliser would be misleading; resolving the normaliser API is left for a follow-up release.

### Added — Four-method entropy API and adaptive differential entropy

- **`method='differential'` on `entropyExpTens` / `entropy_exp_tens`.** Adaptive nested-grid evaluation of the differential entropy $\hat h = H_\text{disc} + \log_b(\Delta\text{-volume})$, the grid-independent continuous quantity that compares densities of different cardinality or spread on the same scale (unlike normalised Shannon, which is grid-dependent). The span auto-derives per group from `centres ± truncation_sigmas * sigma` (non-periodic) or `[0, period]` (periodic); the grid doubles from a sample-per-sigma initial resolution until successive Richardson-extrapolated estimates fall below tolerance, anchored at $\max(\exp(-\text{truncation\_sigmas}^2 / 2), 10^{-12})$. Richardson extrapolation upgrades the bin-integration's $O(\Delta^2)$ rate to $O(\Delta^4)$, making multi-dimensional convergence feasible without exhausting `grid_limit`. Errors at `sigma=0` (the continuous form diverges). Currently restricted to single-density input (scalar density, raw scalar SA / MA); list and batched input forms raise `NotImplementedError`. Bin-integration core via per-axis Φ-difference contractions is shared with the discrete methods, so the same density paths are exercised.

- **`method='normalized'` (alias `'normalised'`) on `entropyExpTens` / `entropy_exp_tens`.** Explicit name for the Pielou-style ratio $H / \log_b(N)$ in $[0, 1]$. Equivalent to `method='shannon'` with `normalize=True` but independent of the legacy boolean kwarg, and reproduces the values reported in Milne et al. (2017) and Smit et al. (2019). Accepts the British spelling as an alias via `_canonicalize_method`.

- **`σ=0` guards on continuous methods.** Both `method='differential'` and `method='renyi2'` now error at `sigma=0` with an informative message pointing users to `method='shannon'` or `method='normalized'` for categorical entropy. The guard reads sigma from any density-object form (`ExpTensDensity`, `MaetDensity`, `WindowedMaetDensity`).

### Changed — Default kernel truncation is now `6` (breaking numerical change); one-shot hint removed

- **`truncation_sigmas` / `truncationSigmas` factory default is now `6`, not `Inf`.** Default-configured output from every centres-path consumer (`evalExpTens`, `cosSimExpTens` on Bulger's method, `entropyExpTens`, `spectralEntropy`, `templateHarmonicity`, `virtualPitches`) is therefore a ~6-significant-figure approximation of the untruncated result rather than bit-identical to v2.1. The worst-case relative error (~$10^{-6}$ to $10^{-7}$ for `centres`, smaller for the Möbius realisation) falls at low-density query points; near the density's bulk it is far smaller, and for the perceptual-modelling regimes the toolbox targets the approximation is well below any perceptually or statistically meaningful threshold. The change is motivated purely by speed: the grid-bucket truncation is a large win (tens to >100× over the untruncated path at large $K$ in the pruning regime), and `6` sits near the knee of the speed/accuracy curve, where the retained-neighbour count scaling as $k^{r}$ means smaller $k$ is materially faster at higher tensor order. **To recover exact v2.1 numerics**, set `truncation_sigmas` / `truncationSigmas` to `math.inf` / `Inf` — per call, or globally via the defaults API. Cross-language Python↔MATLAB parity continues to be verified at $\le 10^{-12}$ on this exact path; the default path is deterministic and identical across languages, so parity is preserved there too, only against the truncated value rather than the untruncated one.

- **The one-shot kernel-evaluation hint has been replaced by a first-use truncation notice.** The old hint existed to steer users off the (former) untruncated default *onto* truncation; with truncation now the default its premise is inverted. In its place, the toolbox issues a one-time-per-session warning the first time a kernel evaluation resolves the truncation default at its factory value of 6 — giving the worst-case error versus the exact result at 4, 5, and 6 sigma (about ~1e-3, ~1e-5, and ~2e-8 respectively) and pointing to `truncation_sigmas=inf` for exact output. It fires from the defaults getter (`mptDefaults('truncationSigmas')` / `get_default('truncation_sigmas')`), through which every kernel-evaluating path resolves the default, so it appears on first use regardless of which function the script calls. It is issued as a warning (identifier `mpt:truncationDefault` in MATLAB, category `mpt.TruncationDefaultWarning` in Python) so it goes to stderr and is suppressible through the standard warning machinery, rather than printed to stdout where it could interleave with a script's own output. It is deliberately **not** gated by `show_hints` / `showHints` — it always gets its single showing — and it stays silent only after that first showing, once the user sets an explicit truncation value, or throughout the test suite. The `show_hints` / `showHints` flag now governs only the toolbox's dispatch-decision messages.



- **The toolbox-wide default of `n_points_per_dim=1200` has been dropped** to `None` in `entropy_exp_tens`. `method='shannon'` and `method='normalized'` now require the caller to pass an explicit grid resolution; a missing value raises `TypeError` with a message pointing to either supplying `n_points_per_dim` or switching to a grid-free method (`'differential'` or `'renyi2'`). The previous silent default masked grid-fragility bugs — the right grid resolution is density- and sigma-dependent — and forcing the choice up front protects callers from accidentally relying on a value tuned for a different regime. The check fires after input-form validation, so the more-specific "extra positional", "spectrum with density", and "precision with density" errors continue to surface when both apply. The check is bypassed by the continuous methods (`differential` adapts its own grid, `renyi2` is analytical).

### Changed — `spectral_entropy` default method (breaking semantic change)

- **`spectralEntropy` / `spectral_entropy` default switched from `method='shannon'` (with `normalize=True`) to `method='differential'`.** The new default returns the adaptive differential entropy $\hat h$ — a different quantity in different units, not a fourth-decimal numerical shift. ### Changed — `nTupleEntropy` `sigmaSpace = 'position'` now exact at all `n`

- **The `'position'` mode is now the exact correlated model for every `n`, superseding the v2.1.0 marginal-matched approximation.** Under positional uncertainty, adjacent first differences share an event, so the `n` steps of a tuple carry covariance $\sigma^2\,\mathrm{tridiag}(2, -1)$ (variance $2\sigma^2$ per step, $-\sigma^2$ between neighbours). v2.1.0 approximated this at $n \ge 2$ by widening each step to $\sigma_{\text{eff}} = \sigma\sqrt{2}$ with slots treated as independent, emitting a one-time `marginal-matched` warning. v2.2 instead binds the $n + 1$ underlying events and takes the bound window relative; the relative projection of the isotropic positional jitter onto the within-window difference space reproduces $\sigma^2\,\mathrm{tridiag}(2, -1)$ exactly from isotropic kernels, so no non-diagonal kernel covariance is needed (contrary to the v2.1.0 note that anticipated one). The Python `marginal-matched` `UserWarning` and the MATLAB `nTupleEntropy:positionApprox` warning are **removed**.
- **Numerical change at `sigma > 0` with `sigmaSpace = 'position'`.** Values change for all `n`: at $n \ge 2$ they are now exact rather than marginal-matched, and at $n = 1$ they are reported on the relative-quotient grid rather than via the $\sigma\sqrt{2}$ interval equivalence, so the v2.1.0 identity (`'position'` at $\sigma$ equalling `'interval'` at $\sigma\sqrt{2}$) no longer holds. `sigma = 0` is unchanged (both flags give the published integer-step histogram of Milne 2015bc / Milne & Dean 2016), and `sigmaSpace = 'interval'` is unchanged at every `sigma`.

### Added — Ragged-K hybrid for MA Möbius inner product

- **Per-event safe/unsafe partition in the MA per-attribute IP matrix.** Replaces the previous zero-pad-everything fallback for ragged $K_{\text{eff}}$ inputs. Each event is classified as "safe" or "unsafe" on each attribute by the $K_{\text{eff}, n, a} - r_a \ge 2$ rule (see Overview). Safe-vs-safe pairs flow through the vectorised batched Möbius-method evaluator with within-safe-group zero-padding to a common width; the waste factor $K_{\max} / \overline{K}$ (the ratio of group-maximum $K_{\text{eff}}$ to group-mean $K_{\text{eff}}$, which measures padded slots per useful slot) is minimised by the restriction to the more uniform safe group. Pairs involving any unsafe event flow through `mobius.innerProductDirectAbsSA` (Python `_inner_product_direct_abs_sa`), which enumerates ordered $r$-tuples directly and contracts without a Möbius alternating sum — exact for any $K \ge r$, no cancellation possible. The four submatrices (safe-vs-safe, safe-vs-unsafe, unsafe-vs-safe, unsafe-vs-unsafe) stitch into the full $(N_x, N_y)$ IP matrix without double coverage. The MA dispatcher no longer routes on the presence of NaN entries, since the Möbius-method wrapper handles them natively.

- **K-grouped batched direct enumeration for the unsafe path.** Unsafe-involved pairs are grouped by $(K_{\text{eff}, x}, K_{\text{eff}, y})$ value: each homogeneous sub-block becomes a single vectorised tensor contraction (`np.einsum` in Python; two reduction steps in MATLAB) covering all events with those exact $K_{\text{eff}}$ values, with mathematical coverage `(unsafe_x, all_y) ∪ (safe_x, unsafe_y)`. Uniform-$K_{\text{eff}}$ workloads incur only a few empty-set checks and a single grouped contraction; variable-$K_{\text{eff}}$ workloads dispatch to one contraction per $(K_{\text{eff}, x}, K_{\text{eff}, y})$ subset. See `mobius.maPerAttrInnerMatrix` (MATLAB) / `_ma_per_attr_inner_matrix` (Python) and `tests/test_ma_per_attr_hybrid.{m,py}`.

The weight return changed from a rolling-product row to a 1-by-$n$ cell array parallel to `pBound` (numerically equivalent for $K_{a, n} = 1$ via lazy multiplication), so consumers can apply per-attribute weight semantics. ### Added — `translateAttributes` / `translate_attributes`

- **New pre-tensor preprocessing helper.** Shifts selected attributes' values by a chosen offset. Takes the `pAttr` list one would otherwise feed to `buildExpTens` and returns a transformed `pAttrTranslated` list with the same shape conventions; the output feeds directly into `buildExpTens` without further massaging. Sits in the preprocessing family alongside `differenceEvents` and `bindEvents`, but is per-attribute and event-local (no cross-event correspondence) whereas its siblings are cross-event. The primitive is per-attribute; broadcast-within-group (translating a whole group as a unit — the transposition / time-shift case for pitch / time groups respectively) is a common case expressed by setting the affected rows of the offset block equal.

- **Sliding-comparison primitive.** Provides the **pre-tensor** route to a sliding comparison along an attribute axis: for each candidate offset $\mu$ on a sweep grid, translate the events and compute a similarity against an un-shifted reference, then read off the peak. Complements the **post-tensor** `windowedSimilarity` route. The two routes differ in two ways: locality (pre-tensor uses the query's intrinsic support; post-tensor uses an externally chosen window, decoupling the comparison region's scale from the query) and normalisation (pre-tensor preserves the symmetric cosine and bounds values in $[0, 1]$; post-tensor is magnitude-aware by default, with values that can exceed $1$ and asymmetric query/context roles). On periodic groups both routes are well-defined and operationally interchangeable for whole-density alignment; on relative groups only the post-tensor route is available, since pre-tensor translation is a no-op there.

- **Group-geometry semantics.** Absolute non-periodic groups (`is_per[g] = False`): `value + mu`. Absolute periodic groups (`is_per[g] = True` with `periods[g] > 0`): `value + mu`, unwrapped — the wrapped periodic Gaussian kernel of `buildExpTens` is invariant under any additive shift by a multiple of $P$, so no canonical wrap of the translated values is required. **Relative groups**: structural no-op (a uniform shift cancels in every within-tuple difference); the function emits `translateAttributes:noOpRelative` (MATLAB) / `TranslateAttributesNoOpWarning` (Python) and leaves the group unchanged. The `is_rel`, `is_per`, and `periods` arguments mirror `buildExpTens` in shape, so the caller can pass the same per-group geometry flags they would pass to the downstream `buildExpTens` call without translation; `is_per` and `periods` are accepted for signature parallelism but are not consulted by `translateAttributes` itself, since translation outputs unwrapped values regardless of the group's periodicity.

- **Multi-slot attributes ($K_{a, n} > 1$) handled without ambiguity.** Every value in the per-event multiset is shifted by the same offset, so unlike post-tensor windowing — where a per-event scalar weight would have to summarise multiple values' distances from a localising centre — translation has no analogous obstruction at $K_{a, n} > 1$.

- **Orientation-based offset grammar.** `offsets` accepts two forms with a single shared convention: rows index attributes, columns index sweep positions, and row count must be exactly 1 (broadcast across all attributes) or $A$ (per-attribute). The **numeric form** is a single numeric block whose permitted shapes are scalar / $1 \times 1$ (broadcast, no sweep); $1 \times M$ row (broadcast, $M$-sweep); $A \times 1$ column (per-attribute, single translation); and $A \times M$ matrix (per-attribute, $M$-sweep). The **container form** is a `{group_index: value}` dict in Python, or a $1$-by-$G$ cell in MATLAB; each per-group value follows the same orientation convention with $n_g$ (the number of attributes in group $g$) playing the role of $A$ — covering mixed-per-group layouts (broadcast within one group, per-attribute within another) in a single call. Groups omitted (Python: missing key; MATLAB: empty `[]` cell) are not translated. All entries carrying $M > 1$ across the call must agree on $M$; scalar and 1-column entries broadcast across the sweep. NaN entries in any numeric block skip the corresponding `(attribute, column)` cell. When any input implies a sweep or any 2-D shape is used, the output is a length-$M$ list (Python) / $1$-by-$M$ cell (MATLAB) of per-attribute blocks (matrix-mode); otherwise a single per-attribute block. The matrix-mode output is the natural feed into the raw-MA scalar-vs-list mode of `cosSimExpTens` for a one-call sliding-comparison sweep. When multiple sweep columns carry finite entries on a relative-group attribute, the no-op warning is emitted at most once per call rather than once per column.

- **API signatures.** Python:

  ```python
  # broadcast, single translation
  p_translated = translate_attributes(
      p_attr, groups, 6.0, is_rel, is_per, periods,
  )

  # broadcast sweep — 1 x M row (or 1-D ndarray)
  p_swept = translate_attributes(
      p_attr, groups, np.array([[0.0, 5.0, 10.0]]),
      is_rel, is_per, periods,
  )                                                   # list of length M

  offsets_mat = np.vstack([pitch_grid, time_grid])    # (A, M)
  p_swept = translate_attributes(
      p_attr, groups, offsets_mat, is_rel, is_per, periods,
  )

  # mixed per-group layout — dict form
  p_swept = translate_attributes(
      p_attr, groups,
      {0: 5.0,                              # group 0: broadcast scalar
       1: np.array([[0.1], [-0.2], [0.0]])},# group 1: per-attribute column
      is_rel, is_per, periods,
  ```

### Added — Matrix-valued kernel covariances (anisotropic kernels)

- **`sigma` accepts an $r \times r$ covariance matrix.** On an ordered (`isSym = false`), absolute (`isRel = false`), non-periodic (`isPer = false`), non-nested attribute whose tuple is its whole multiset ($r = K$), the per-attribute `sigma` of `buildExpTens`, `evalExpTens`, `cosSimExpTens`, `entropyExpTens` (all four methods), `windowedSimilarity`, and `windowedEntropy` may be a symmetric positive-definite matrix $\Sigma$ instead of a scalar: the Gaussian kernel becomes $\exp(-\delta^{\top} \Sigma^{-1} \delta / 2)$, and the inner product of two densities sharing $\Sigma$ depends on centre differences through $\exp(-\delta^{\top} \Sigma^{-1} \delta / 4)$. A scalar `sigma` remains a standard deviation; a matrix is the covariance (squared units). Validation always errors: a non-square, wrongly sized, asymmetric, non-finite, or non-positive-definite matrix raises (one attempted Cholesky factorization is the definiteness test), as does a matrix supplied on a symmetric, relative, periodic, nested, or $r < K$ attribute, on `'spectrum'` input, or on values containing NaN pads. Exact common-shift invariance remains the province of `isRel = true` — a matrix covariance expresses its graded counterpart, and an infinite shift variance is rejected rather than approximated.

- **Whitening implementation.** With the Cholesky factorization $\Sigma = R R^{\top}$, values and query points are transformed once by $R^{-1}$ and every downstream computation (density evaluation, inner products, entropies, truncation and precision controls) runs unchanged on whitened coordinates at unit `sigma`. The Gaussian normalisation constant acquires $\det(\Sigma)^{-1/2}$ (`normalize = 'gaussian'` / `'pdf'`; it cancels in cosine similarity), and the continuous entropies acquire the additive change-of-variables constant $\tfrac{1}{2} \log \det(\Sigma)$. Inner products require the same covariance on both operands (per attribute); a mismatch raises.

- **`intervalKernelCov` / `interval_kernel_cov`.** Constructor for the covariance of an ordered tuple of $r$ consecutive differences (intervals) of $r + 1$ underlying positions: $\Sigma = \mathrm{sd\_position}^2 \, D D^{\top} + \mathrm{sd\_interval}^2 \, I + \mathrm{sd\_shift}^2 \, \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}$, with $D$ the first-differencing map (so $D D^{\top}$ is tridiagonal: $2$ on the diagonal, $-1$ on the first off-diagonals). The three independently specified, added terms are: uncertainty on the underlying positions, propagated through the shared endpoints (the exact counterpart of `sigmaSpace = 'position'` in `nTupleEntropy`); uncertainty on each interval itself, independent across intervals (`sigmaSpace = 'interval'`); and graded tolerance for a common shift of the whole tuple — a transposition for pitch intervals, a tempo change for log inter-onset intervals — whose precision tends to the relative-mode projector as `sd_shift` grows, `isRel = true` being the exact limit. In the time reading the first two terms are the two levels of the Wing & Kristofferson (1973) timing model (motor delays on onsets; central timekeeper variance on intervals). All three arguments are standard deviations in the attribute's own coordinates and are squared internally; the all-zero and `sd_shift`-alone (rank-one) cases raise as singular, and $r = 1$ is rejected in both languages because a $1 \times 1$ covariance is indistinguishable from a scalar `sigma` in MATLAB (pass the equivalent standard deviation instead). A future release will route `nTupleEntropy`'s `sigmaSpace = 'position'` mode through this covariance.

- **`isSym` on `windowedSimilarity` / `windowedEntropy`.** New keyword carrying the per-attribute symmetry vector of the flat surface through the sweep's internal builds and comparisons (`None` / omitted keeps the unordered default). Required, in particular, for ordered attributes carrying a matrix-valued kernel covariance. Mutually exclusive with `specs`, whose nesting carries its own per-level sym.

- **Post-tensor windowing guarded.** `windowTensor` and `windowedTensorSimilarity` reject densities built with a matrix-valued kernel covariance: their stored coordinates are whitened, so a window or offset specified in the attribute's original coordinates would be applied in the wrong frame. The raw sliding comparisons (`windowedSimilarity`, `windowedEntropy`) translate and window the raw events before each density is built, and accept matrix covariances throughout.

- **Degenerate nested attributes accepted (and flattened).** A matrix-valued kernel covariance is accepted on a nested attribute when the nesting is *degenerate* --- two levels, every group a singleton read whole, inner level absolute, outer level reading all groups --- the form produced by `bindEvents` / `bind_events` over flat single-slot events. Such an attribute is order-isomorphic to the flat ordered tuple over the same value matrix (identical densities), so the `specs` build path flattens it automatically before whitening (`internal.flattenDegenerateNestedSpec` / `flatten_degenerate_nested_spec`); outer-level `sym` / `rel` flags carry through to the standard mode-constraint checks and are rejected with the canonical messages. A matrix sigma on a *flat* spec passed via `specs` is likewise accepted, matching the positional form. An all-scalar cell `sigma` in the MATLAB `specs` form is coerced to numeric, likewise mirroring the positional path. Non-degenerate nesting on a matrix-sigma attribute errors. `demos/demo_tempoInvariance` / `demos/demo_tempo_invariance` builds its trigram carrier this way (`differenceEvents` then `bindEvents`), expressing the rel kernel via `relOuter = true` --- the spec-native form of the common-shift quotient.

### Added — Demo: handling repeated pitches under interval-scale and tempo invariance

- **`demos/demo_repetitionHandling` / `demos/demo_repetition_handling`.** Matching a melodic contour up to a uniform scaling of its intervals carries each pitch step as two attributes --- sign, and the logarithm of the step magnitude, the bound log-magnitude tuple read relative (`relOuter`) so a common scaling becomes a quotiented common shift. A repeated pitch has step 0, which has no logarithm, so something must be done with repetitions first, and the demo walks the same melody (the Ode to Joy opening, whose repetitions are non-uniformly placed) through the three treatments: *excise* (drop each zero-step event with its inter-onset interval; a repetition leaves no trace, so the melody is identified with its repetition-free, closed-up form), *prolong* (gather each run of equal consecutive pitches into one event before differencing; the repetition survives as duration, at the cost of identifying a re-articulated note with a held one), and *count* (prolong, plus a per-event attribute holding the number of gathered onsets; re-articulation survives as such, and its sigma grades the distinction back toward the prolong identification as it widens). Each treatment runs twice --- inter-onset intervals in beats (tempo-sensitive) and log inter-onset intervals read relative (tempo-invariant) --- and the invariant run exposes the confound the sensitive one hides: where repetition is uniform (every note repeated equally), closing the repetitions up *is* a tempo change, and only the count attribute, dimensionless and so invariant to both scalings for free, keeps the two apart. Exact quotients only (`relOuter` flags); the graded counterparts (the `sdShift` ridge of `intervalKernelCov`, tending to rel in the limit) are the subject of `demo_tempoInvariance`.

### Added — `weightEvents` / `weight_events`

- **New pre-MAET preprocessing helper.** Computes a per-event window factor from values of an input attribute and writes the factor into the weight slot of a (possibly different) target attribute. Joins `differenceEvents`, `bindEvents`, and `translateAttributes` as the fourth pre-MAET preprocessing primitive — the per-event weight-modifying primitive, complementing the per-event value-modifying `translateAttributes` and the cross-event `differenceEvents` and `bindEvents`. Returns a three-tuple `(pAttr, wOut, groups)` that feeds directly into `buildExpTens`.

- **Input attribute and target attribute are separate.** The signature takes `inputAttr` (the attribute supplying values to the window function) and `targetAttr` (the attribute whose weight slot receives the factor) as separate scalar indices. They may be equal (the input attribute weights itself — pitch-band selection of pitch events, for example) or distinct (the cross-attribute case, where e.g. time provides the values and pitch receives the factor). Multi-axis windowing — windowing on time *and* on register, both targeting pitch — is expressed as a sequence of `weightEvents` calls with the same `targetAttr`; the multiplicative composition into the target's weight slot makes this a clean idiom.

- **K-restriction on the input.** `inputAttr` must reference an attribute with $K = 1$ (single value per event); multi-value input attributes are rejected. The per-event factor is therefore always a $(1, N)$ row, broadcast across the target attribute's $K_{\text{target}}$ slots in `buildExpTens`'s kernel product.

- **Window family.** The window is specified by three numeric parameters: a centre $c$, a width $w$ (the standard deviation, in absolute units of the input attribute), and a shape parameter $\gamma \in [0, 1]$. The factor is the peak-normalised convolution of a rectangle of half-width $w\sqrt{3\gamma}$ and a Gaussian of standard deviation $w\sqrt{1 - \gamma}$, so the window's total variance is $w^2$ for every value of $\gamma$. The extremes are pure Gaussian ($\gamma = 0$) and pure rectangle ($\gamma = 1$, with half-width $w\sqrt{3}$); intermediate $\gamma$ interpolates continuously between them. Periodic input groups wrap the difference $\delta = v - c$ used inside the factor to $[-P/2, P/2]$ before evaluation; stored values stay raw and the kernel handles value-axis periodicity downstream.

- **`deleteInput` (mandatory, no default).** A keyword-only boolean. When `true` and `inputAttr != targetAttr`, removes the input attribute from the returned `pAttr`, `wOut`, and `groups`; if the input was the sole member of its group, that group disappears and higher group indices are decremented to keep numbering contiguous. When `false`, the input attribute is preserved in the output. `deleteInput = true` paired with `inputAttr == targetAttr` raises an error (deleting the input would discard the factor just written to it). Callers must specify the flag; there is no default. The auto-delete pattern is the standard convention for windowed-entropy and local-mass workflows where the input attribute (typically time) provides the scaffolding for the window and is no longer needed downstream once its values have been transferred to the target's weight slot.

- **Centre-shift identity.** With `inputAttr == targetAttr`, `weightEvents` followed by `translateAttributes` on the same attribute equals translation first then `weightEvents` at a shifted centre $c + \mu$ (the residual $v - c$ is preserved). The identity underlies the windowed-entropy and pre-tensor sliding-comparison constructions.

- **Canonical composition.** The pattern `weightEvents` (with `deleteInput=true`, `inputAttr` typically time, `targetAttr` typically pitch) → `buildExpTens` → `entropyExpTens` is the windowed-entropy construction (entropy of the local slice of a density visible through the window). Conceptually documented in USER_GUIDE §3.7 (Pre-MAET processing).

### Changed — `width` semantics on `weightEvents` / `weight_events`; added `sd` and `width` Name-Value pair API

- **The single `width` positional/scalar argument is replaced by two Name-Value (Python: keyword-only) arguments, `sd` and `width`, of which exactly one must be supplied.** Both specify the same underlying scale on different terms: `sd` is the window's standard deviation (the parameter the previous API implicitly used under its variance-normalised parameterisation), while `width` is the *full support* of the rectangle at `shape = 1`. The conversion is `sd = width / (2 sqrt(3))`. The two conventions exist because each is the natural way to specify the kind of kernel a particular analysis is built around: Gaussian users typically think in standard deviations (`sd`), rectangle users typically think in full supports (`width`). Across the full `shape` family the SD is held constant regardless of which parameter the caller supplied (variance-normalised behaviour is preserved), so the only effect of the parameter choice is the numerical value the user types. This eliminates a foot-gun in the previous API: writing `width = 0.25` to mean "rect of total support 0.25 QN" silently gave a rectangle with cutoff at $\pm 0.25 \sqrt 3 \approx \pm 0.433$ and captured roughly three times the intended events. Under the new API, `width = 0.25` means exactly that the rectangle covers $[-0.125, +0.125]$ as written. The previous helper function (introduced briefly in earlier development) is removed: with the new API no conversion helper is needed because the user names the parameter in the unit they're thinking in. Validation: supplying neither `sd` nor `width` (or supplying both) raises `TypeError` (Python) / `'weightEvents:sdWidthXor'` (MATLAB).

### Fixed — Ordered ([sym] = 0) attributes no longer over-counted by the dispatch memory guards

- **The dispatch working-set estimates now count an ordered attribute's enumerated tuples as $C(K_a, r_a)$ rather than $K_a!/(K_a - r_a)!$.** The actual enumeration (`_enum_flat_attr` in Python; the MATLAB build's twin) admits every ordering of every combination only on an *unordered* (`[sym] = 1`) attribute; on an ordered attribute the perm side equals the comb side, so at $r_a = K_a$ — the flat ordered tuples that `bindEvents` / `bind_events` over single-slot events flattens to — each event holds exactly *one* tuple. Three estimates priced every attribute at the unordered count: the forced-Bulger feasibility guard on the inner-product path (Python `_guard_forced_bulger_feasible_ma`), the soft working-set budget in the MA inner-product selector, and the joint working-set estimate on the eval path (`_estimate_ma_joint_working_set_bytes` / `internal.estimateMaJointWorkingSetBytes`). The over-count is a factor of $r_a!$ per ordered attribute, so a three-attribute bound 9-tuple density — one tuple per attribute per event — was priced at $9!^3 \approx 4.8 \times 10^{16}$ tuples per side and rejected by the Python inner-product guard with `SingleImageInfeasibleError` ("would need ~$10^{25}$ GB"), although the actual computation is a single kernel entry. All three estimates now take the per-attribute `[sym]` flags (omitted, every attribute is treated as unordered — the conservative, larger count — so callers outside the density paths are unchanged); the cosine and eval callers thread the density's own flags. The cost-model *pricing* functions (`_predict_pairwise_kernel_size` / `predictPairwiseKernelSize` and the Möbius centres term) retain the unordered count: ordered densities are force-routed to the pairwise/centres path after the selector runs, so their price there affects no reachable routing decision, and the calibrated constants are left untouched. MATLAB's MA inner-product selector carries no forced-Bulger guard (a pre-existing divergence from Python, unchanged by this fix), and both languages' eval selectors route ordered attributes to the centres path on a hard rule before the guarded feasibility branch, so the user-visible symptom was Python-only and inner-product-only. Regression tests: `test_ma_orbit.py` (ordered attributes pass the guard; one unordered attribute still trips it; a bound ordered 9-tuple density with a relative attribute matches the closed-form single-pair cosine to 1e-10) and `test_ma_eval_cost_model.py` (estimator counts; a bound ordered 11-tuple routes to centres and evaluates) in Python; `test_dispatch_oom_guard.m` cases 6--8 in MATLAB. Alongside the fix, the dispatch-decision announce on the multi-attribute cosine path now labels the ordered case `'bulger (direct on ordered attributes)'` in both languages: on an ordered attribute the tuple-pair path has no permutation expansion to exploit (the perm side equals the comb side), so Bulger's combinations-vs-permutations organisation never runs there and the printed label previously overstated what ran. The `method` keyword's `'bulger'` token is unchanged.

### Fixed — Ordered ([sym] = 0) densities no longer routed to the Möbius evaluator

- **`evalExpTens` / `eval_exp_tens` now keep the joint-centres path for any flat attribute that is ordered (`[sym] = 0`) at $r > 1$, and reject an explicit `method='mobius'` on such a density with a clear error.** The Möbius decomposition rewrites the distinct-slot-tuple sum as an alternating sum over set partitions of the *slot indices*, which counts every ordering of each block; the orbit collapse then exploits the joint slot-permutation symmetry. Both steps presuppose the symmetrised reading. On an ordered attribute that symmetry is exactly what `[sym] = 0` removes, so the partition sum realises the symmetrised tuple set — a *different* density, not the same one computed faster. Previously the cost model could route such a density to the Möbius evaluator, which returned the symmetrised values silently: at $K = 4$, $\sigma = 2$, $r = 2$, the ordered density evaluated at a mirrored query pair returned a ratio of $1.00$ (symmetric) where the correct ordered ratio is $65.9$, and at $r = 3$ a ratio of $1.00$ against a correct $2.5 \times 10^5$. The error was silent — a wrong number, not a failure — and grew with the query count, since the cost model favours Möbius on large query batches while small batches stayed on the centres path and were correct. Downstream, brute-force grid integrals of ordered densities (and any Rényi-2 cross-check built on them) inherited the symmetrised values. The analytical Rényi-2 path was never affected: it already routes ordered flat attributes at $r > 1$ to the direct per-attribute numerical inner matrix. $r = 1$ attributes are exempt (`[sym]` is vacuous at a single slot) and nested attributes were already held on the centres path. Both languages; the predicate is shared as `internal.hasOrderedAttr` / `_has_ordered_attr`.



- **The pure-rectangle window (`shape = 1`) now uses a half-open support $[c - W/2,\, c + W/2)$ — lower edge included, upper edge excluded — with a floating-point-robust boundary test, in place of a closed $[c - W/2,\, c + W/2]$.** A closed interval is inconsistent at the two edges in opposite directions: a window of full support $N \cdot \mathrm{IOI}$ centred on a pulse includes both boundary pulses and so always spans an *odd* number of pulses (widths $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ collapse to pulse counts $1, 3, 3, 5, 5$, each even width silently duplicating the next odd one), while a centre placed midway between two pulses leaves the flanking events on — or, under rounding, just outside — the edges, giving a zero-mass window. The half-open rule yields exactly $N$ pulses for full support $N \cdot \mathrm{IOI}$ at every $N$ and removes the between-pulse degeneracy. The change is local to the rectangular-weight branch; the analytical window inner product in `windowTensor` / `window_tensor` is unaffected, since it integrates a continuous window and does not depend on the edge membership of discrete events. Gaussian and intermediate `shape` values are unchanged. Regression tests in `test_rect_window_and_renyi2_degenerate` (Python) and the `weightEvents` block of `test_maet` (MATLAB) cover the width-to-pulse-count mapping and the between-pulse-centre case.

### Fixed — Zero-weight tuple prune in cell-mass entropy path

- **`_cell_masses_ma_absolute` / `_cell_masses_sa_absolute` (Python) and `localCellMassesMAAbsolute` / `localCellMassesSAAbsolute` (MATLAB)** now auto-prune tuples with `w_j == 0` before building the per-axis erf-difference matrices. Mirrors the pre-existing eval-path prune in `_tensor/eval.py`; without it, the absolute-mode Shannon-on-grid path (used directly by `method='shannon'`, `'normalized'`, and indirectly by the adaptive `'differential'` evaluator) iterated over every upstream tuple including those that `weightEvents` had hard-zeroed under `truncation_sigmas`. On long sequences with a narrow window this could OOM, even though zero-weight tuples contribute exactly zero to the einsum. Mathematically exact (the einsum result is unchanged); numerically identical to a hand-prune at the call site to within ~1e-10 abs for `'shannon'` and ~1e-8 abs for `'differential'`. Regression tests in `test_cell_mass_zero_weight_prune` cover the parity check and the bounded-completion property under a Gaussian window over a 1000-event sequence.

### Fixed — Zero-weight event prune in the inner-product / total-mass paths (Rényi-2, cosine)

- **`entropyExpTens` (`method='renyi2'`) and `cosSimExpTens` now drop dead (zero- or NaN-weight) events at the density level before the per-attribute inner-product and total-mass work.** The drop goes through a new density-level live-event view: Python exposes it as a `pruned()` method on `ExpTensDensity` / `MaetDensity`; MATLAB as the `internal.prunedExpTens` helper (density structs carry no methods, so the parity is a function rather than a method). Both share one liveness predicate — a weight contributes iff it is finite and of nonzero magnitude. The live rule: for SA an element is live iff its weight is finite and nonzero; for MA an event is live iff *every* attribute has at least one finite, nonzero slot in that event's column (the per-attribute factors multiply, so an all-zero or all-NaN column kills the event, while a partly-zero column does not). This is the sibling, one level up, of the cell-mass tuple prune above: that prune drops zero-weight *tuples* inside the grid path; this one drops whole *events* before the inner-product / total-mass reduction. It is needed for the same reason the cell-mass case was — `maPerAttrInnerMatrix` / `_ma_per_attr_inner_matrix` already prunes per attribute, but `weightEvents` writes its window factor to only the target attribute, so an event hard-zeroed through one attribute under `truncation_sigmas` remains present (all-ones) on the others, and the event-level kill is invisible to a per-attribute prune; the Rényi-2 and cosine paths therefore walked the full event set. Mathematically exact: a dead event contributes exactly zero to every inner product and total mass, so cosine similarity, Rényi-2 entropy, and all quantities normalised by total mass are unchanged — this is a performance fix, not a numerical one. Measured on an MA density of 4488 events windowed down to 350 live events, MA Rényi-2 returns the identical value (−1.911267 nats) in roughly 17 ms versus roughly 2300 ms unpruned; SA Rényi-2 and cosine similarity are bit-for-bit identical with the dead events present versus removed. `pruned()` / `internal.prunedExpTens` returns the density unchanged when nothing is dead, so the common un-windowed path pays only a single mask scan. Three scope boundaries are deliberate: `buildExpTens` stays faithful (it keeps every event, so a density's event count never couples to the runtime `truncation_sigmas` setting); `evalExpTens` keeps its own tuple-level prune, a genuinely finer post-expansion granularity that the event-level view does not subsume; and `windowedSimilarity` is unaffected, since it applies its window analytically inside the inner-product integral and so never materialises discrete zero-weight events (locality there is already handled by `truncation_sigmas`). Regression tests in `test_event_prune` cover the liveness rule (including the partly-zero-column case that must stay live and the all-zero / all-NaN column that kills), the self-return fast path when nothing is dead, Rényi-2 and cosine invariance to dead events in both SA and MA, a realistic `weight_events` truncation case (auto-prune versus manual upstream prune, plus bounded completion on a 3000-event windowed density), and the fully-dead-density edge cases.

### Fixed — `renyi2` returns NaN for a zero-mass density

- **`entropyExpTens` (`method='renyi2'`) now returns `NaN` for a degenerate (zero-mass, or otherwise non-finite-and-positive) density, in place of raising.** A zero-mass density — every weight zero, or a windowed sweep centre with no event in support — has $\langle T, T\rangle = 0$ and $Z = 0$ exactly, so its collision entropy is undefined. Returning `NaN` (the value a windowed sweep already wants at out-of-support centres) lets sweep-style callers read the result directly rather than wrapping each evaluation in a `try`/`except`. The check is a single finite-and-positive guard applied uniformly across the SA, MA, and ordered (`[sym] = 0`) paths, plus the zero-mass (fully pruned, $N = 0$) short circuit. The investigation that prompted this confirmed no orbit-Möbius collision defect behind the earlier failures: every case was a genuinely empty window, not a cancellation in the alternating sum. A second-method numerical fallback for a hypothetical finite-precision cancellation is deliberately out of scope — the orbit and pairwise self-inner-products use different normalisation conventions in relative mode, so a fallback would substitute a differently-scaled value rather than recover the correct one. Regression tests in `test_rect_window_and_renyi2_degenerate` and `test_event_prune` (both languages) cover SA and MA zero-mass returns and the forced non-positive / non-finite inner-product cases.

### Added — `circular` flag on `differenceEvents` / `difference_events`

- **Circular event-sequence differencing.** A new `circular` Name-Value (MATLAB) / keyword-only (Python) argument, default `false`, makes the difference operator wrap at the sequence boundary: $\Delta p_a(n) = p_a(n) - p_a(\mathrm{prev}(n))$ with $\mathrm{prev}(1) = N$. Each attribute's output retains $N$ events at every order; no leading-event drop is needed for per-attribute order alignment. Periodic groups are unchanged in their handling — values are emitted as raw signed differences, with the kernel applying the mod-period wrap at evaluation time. The circular variant is the natural companion to `bindEvents`' existing `circular` flag, and is mathematically sensible in the same settings: cyclic event sequences (looped rhythms, ostinati) in which the boundary difference is a genuine inter-event interval rather than an artefact of truncation. Per-event weights propagate as a rolling product with cyclic indexing under `circular = true`, paralleling the non-circular rolling product. Circular mode requires $\max_a k_a < N$ (the wrap consumes the entire cycle at $k = N$); higher orders raise an error.

- **`nTupleEntropy` composition.** `nTupleEntropy` / `n_tuple_entropy` is the canonical composition of circular differencing with circular binding: cyclic first differences over the $N$-cycle of input events, bound at order $L = n$ into $N$ super-events, with the resulting MAET density's entropy reducing to the integer-step $n$-tuple-histogram entropy in the $\sigma \to 0$ limit (Section §2.3 of the JMM manuscript). The toolbox's implementation composes the two primitives directly.

### Added — `cosSimExpTens` / `cos_sim_exp_tens` raw-MA scalar-vs-list mode

- **One-call sweep from the matrix form of `translateAttributes`.** The raw multi-attribute calling form of `cosSimExpTens` accepts a list of `pAttr` blocks (cell-of-cells in MATLAB; list-of-lists in Python) as exactly one of its two `pAttr` operands. The scalar operand is built once internally and reused against each list entry; the list operand is built once per entry. Weights for the list side are shared across every entry — a single `w` value, not a per-entry list. Returns a 1-by-$M$ cell (MATLAB) or 1-D ndarray (Python). Detection examines the first element of the cell / list: a numeric matrix indicates a single `pAttr` (scalar-vs-scalar dispatch); a nested cell or list indicates a list of `pAttr` blocks (scalar-vs-list dispatch). List-vs-list calls are rejected with a directive to use the explicit density-struct list mode instead — list-vs-list is already served by the struct path, and supporting it in the raw form would double the dispatch surface for a use case that path covers cleanly.

- **Eliminates the per-offset `buildExpTens` loop.** A pre-tensor sliding-comparison sweep over $M$ offsets is expressed in two calls: `translateAttributes` with an $A$-by-$M$ matrix produces the swept list; `cosSimExpTens` in scalar-vs-list mode consumes it and returns the similarity profile. Floating-point parity with an explicit per-offset build loop is exact (max $|S_\text{raw} - S_\text{manual}| = 0$ to machine precision on the demo and tests).

### Fixed

- **`evalExpTens` / `eval_exp_tens`** — Fixed the periodic-relative quadratic form $Q$ to use the pairwise-wrap form (Eq 6 of the preprint), matching `cosSimExpTens` and the JMM Eq. 3.4 integral form to $O((\sigma/P)^{\infty})$. Previously the centres-array path used the algebraic-with-outer-wrap form $Q = \sum_k \mathrm{wrap}(D_k)^2 - (\sum_k \mathrm{wrap}(D_k))^2 / r$ inherited from the v1 implementation — a per-component-image approximation that diverges from the pairwise-wrap form when within-tuple pairwise differences cross $\pm P/2$. The same correctness fix was applied to `cosSimExpTens` in v2.0.1; the parallel fix in `evalExpTens` was overlooked at the time and is now applied. At typical perceptual $\sigma/P \le 0.03$ the two forms agree as $O((\sigma/P)^{\infty})$; above the threshold the difference becomes measurable. Affects all SA centres-fast / centres-full eval sites and the MA per-attribute eval sites in both languages; non-periodic and absolute results are unchanged.

### Changed — `differenceEvents` signature and periodicity convention (breaking)

- **Signature.** `differenceEvents(pAttr, w, groups, diffOrders, 'circular', false)`. Four positional arguments plus the `circular` Name-Value (MATLAB) / keyword-only (Python) flag. The v2.1 trailing `periods` positional argument is no longer part of the signature; v2.1 callers passing a `periods` argument will need to drop it (see MIGRATION.md).

- **Periodicity convention.** Differences are emitted as raw signed subtractions regardless of group periodicity. The mod-period wrap on periodic groups is applied by the kernel at MAET-construction time, downstream of `differenceEvents`. This brings the function into line with the toolbox-wide convention that pre-MAET preprocessing primitives produce raw values and the kernel handles wrap downstream (the same convention `translateAttributes` follows on periodic absolute groups, and that `bindEvents` is structurally exempt from since binding doesn't produce arithmetic differences). The kernel's mod-period wrap is exact and produces identical downstream densities to a pre-MAET wrap, so no analysis built on the standard pipeline (`differenceEvents` → `buildExpTens` → MAET consumer) sees a value difference; only analyses that consumed the wrapped pre-MAET values directly are affected.

### Changed — Memory chunking extended to `maPerAttrInnerMatrix` / `_ma_per_attr_inner_matrix`

- **Three previously unchunked $O(K^2 N^2)$ pairwise-differences tensor allocations now respect `kernelChunkBytes`.** The per-attribute inner-product matrix builder (used by every MA Möbius IP call — cosine similarity, Rényi-2 entropy, and the multi-attribute paths of the MAET dispatcher) had three vectorised branches that materialised the full pairwise-differences tensor in a single allocation: the $r = 1$ absolute branch, the $r \ge 2$ absolute safe-pair submatrix, and the relative-periodic pair tensor inside `maPerAttrInnerMatrixRelPer` / `_ma_per_attr_inner_matrix_rel_per`. All three now chunk along the $N_x$ outer axis whenever the single-shot allocation would exceed `kernelChunkBytes`. The fast unchunked path is preserved when budget allows. Bit-identical results to the unchunked path (parity tests under a forced small budget verify this), and unblocks the multi-attribute Möbius IP at large $(N, K)$ — e.g., the BWV~347 16th-note encoding ($N = 272$ events) with 12-partial spectral expansion ($K = 48$) is now usable for windowed multi-attribute analyses where the previous single-shot allocation OOM'd on 8 GB systems.

### Changed — `tensorHarmonicity` Möbius-method rewrite

- **`tensorHarmonicity` now bypasses `buildExpTens` entirely.** Routes through `mobius.evalOrbitRel` (the Möbius method's relative-mode evaluator) for the harmonic-template tensor, with per-chord and per-template caching in batched mode (via `containers.Map` in MATLAB / `dict` in Python). No `buildExpTens` density object is constructed, no centres array is materialised. Output is unchanged at the floating-point level. Removes the previous "consider K_template > 3" warning since the Möbius method handles arbitrary K-template without the centres-array memory footprint.

- **Vectorised u-grid loop in `mobius.evalOrbitRel`.** The relative-mode evaluator integrates over the relative-coordinate axis $u$ (the translation parameter for relative-mode densities): a finite "u-grid" of $N_u$ sample points covers the support window, and the Möbius evaluator processes all u-grid points in a single chunked, batched call to `mobius.evalOrbitAbs`. `mobius.evalOrbitAbs` accepts query arrays of shape `(r, ...)` with arbitrary trailing dimensions; the trailing shape is preserved in the output. Per-block intermediate is `O(m_max · N · N_u · n_q_chunk)`, with chunking along the query axis to bound peak memory at a ~1 GB heuristic budget. Verified in `tests/test_orbit_vectorisation.{m,py}`.

- **Factored (K-free) integrand strategy in `mobius.evalOrbitRel`.** In non-periodic relative mode, each partition block's integrand factor at a u-node separates *exactly* as $\exp(-\mathrm{var}(\delta_B)/2\sigma^2)\, S_m(u + \mathrm{mean}(\delta_B))$, where $S_m(v) = \sum_i w_i^m \exp(-m(v - p_i)^2/2\sigma^2)$ is a query-independent smoothed event distribution at width $\sigma/\sqrt{m}$ and only $r$ distinct block sizes occur. The evaluator tabulates $S_1..S_r$ once (through `gaussianKernelSum`, so `truncation_sigmas` / `kernel_precision` apply uniformly) and reads them back by local quintic (6-point Lagrange) interpolation with a closed-form stencil shared verbatim between MATLAB and Python. Per-query cost drops from $B_r \cdot r \cdot K \cdot N_u$ to $B_r \cdot r \cdot N_u$ after the amortised tabulation. Read-back accuracy is governed by `truncation_sigmas`: the tabulation step targets a relative error at the kernel-truncation floor $\exp(-k^2/2)$, clamped to $[10^{-12}, 10^{-3}]$ ($k = \infty$, the factory default, targets $10^{-12}$; `kernel_precision='single'` floors the target at $10^{-7}$). Error semantics match the rest of the Möbius machinery: read-back error is bounded relative to partition-*term* scales, so the assembled value's relative error degrades by the query's cancellation ratio (as reported by the cancellation-ratio diagnostic) exactly as truncation error and floating-point round-off already do on the direct strategy. A per-call cost gate selects the cheaper strategy (`factored` keyword: auto / force-on / force-off for testing); in periodic relative mode the strategy applies where the variance/mean factorisation survives wrapping — the truncation window $\sqrt{2} k \sigma$ and each query's position span must both fit within half the period — and the direct strategy covers the remaining configurations, where a block's offsets can straddle an image boundary. The eval dispatcher's rel-mode pre-screen now compares centres against the cheaper of the two strategies, so batched relative workloads are no longer routed to centres that the factored Möbius strategy wins (the timing probe retains the final word). Verified in `tests/test_orbit_rel_factored.py` and `tests/test_orbit_vectorisation.m`.

- **Spectral (Fourier) strategy in `mobius.evalOrbitRel`.** A third relative-mode strategy that carries no u-grid. Integrating the partition product over the translation $u$ forces the total mode index of each partition's per-block spectra to zero, so each partition reduces to a constrained coefficient of a product of per-block spectra, and the Fourier coefficients of a Gaussian mixture are available in closed form: $c_m[j] = (\sigma_m \sqrt{2\pi} / L) \exp(-2\pi^2 \sigma_m^2 j^2 / L^2) \sum_i w_i^m e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega j p_i}$, exact for the wrapped mixture on the period $L$. Evaluation dispatches by block count: single-block partitions are the analytic mass $\sum_i w_i^r \cdot \sigma\sqrt{2\pi/r}$; two-block partitions are one-dimensional mode series evaluated through a multiplicative phase ladder; partitions of three or more blocks are total-mode-zero coefficients obtained from cached per-block transforms, one per distinct block, shared across partitions exactly as blocks recur. The mode cutoff is set at machine precision (envelope $\exp(-8.6^2/2) \approx 8 \times 10^{-17}$) rather than at the requested accuracy floor, because the alternating partition sum amplifies per-term error by the query's cancellation ratio; since the mode count grows as $\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}$, machine precision costs about 16% more modes than the $10^{-12}$ floor would. The non-periodic case embeds in a virtual period padded to the truncation floor. Queries are chunked against the `kernelChunkBytes` budget; because the batched transforms differ in batch shape between chunkings, chunk invariance holds to machine precision rather than bitwise. `kernel_precision` does not apply: the coefficients are closed-form in double precision. The cost gate engages the strategy for $r = 2$, $3$, $4$ under `'auto'` above calibrated query-count and $K$ thresholds, and stands down for cancellation-ratio requests (the strategy has no per-node terms matching that diagnostic), outside the periodic validity window, and at $r = 5$, where the factored strategy remains faster at scale. Measured at $K = 300$, source span 3600, $\sigma = 15$, `truncation_sigmas=6`, non-periodic: at $r = 2$, 59 ms versus 1472 ms (direct) for 1000 queries; at $r = 3$, 805 ms versus 2578 ms; at $r = 4$, 1927 ms versus 5432 ms. Accuracy sits at the truncation floor throughout ($\le 1.3 \times 10^{-8}$ at `truncation_sigmas=6`, $\le 6.8 \times 10^{-13}$ at `inf`, over $r = 2\ldots5$ in both modes). The MA eval cost model prices the strategy with a span-aware per-query slope so that batched relative workloads are routed accordingly. Verified in `tests/test_orbit_rel_corr_r2.py` and the spectral-strategy block of `matlab/tests/test_mobius_eval.m`.

- **Spectral (Fourier) strategy for the relative-mode inner product (Python only).** The same partition-decomposition idea that removes the u-grid from `evalOrbitRel` also removes the translation grid from the relative-mode per-attribute inner matrix built by `_ma_per_attr_inner_matrix_rel`. Under a rigid diagonal shift the relative inner product integrates to the absolute one, and transforming along the shift constrains each partition's total mode index to zero, so each event carries a single spectrum $S_n(\xi) = \sum_\pi \mu(\pi) \prod_B A_{|B|}(\eta_B)$ with $A_m(\eta) = \sum_i w_{i,n}^m e^{-\mathrm{i}\eta p_{i,n}}$, and the matrix over event pairs is the Gram matrix of those spectra weighted by the envelope $\exp(-\sigma^2 \sum_s \xi_s^2)$ and scaled by $C_r = r \cdot (\sigma^2 \, d\xi)^{r-1}$. The K-dependence is paid once per event rather than once per pair; the per-pair cost is independent of K. Frequencies are exact on the circle (wrapped-Gaussian coefficients are closed form at any $\sigma/P$); on the line they are spaced $2\pi/L$ for an embedding period covering both sides' spans plus a truncation margin. Hermitian symmetry cuts the working sets in half on two sides: the envelope is even under $\xi \to -\xi$ and $S_n(-\xi) = \mathrm{conj}(S_n(\xi))$ for real Gaussian mixtures, so only the DC point plus one point per $(\xi, -\xi)$ pair is evaluated on the grid (off-DC envelope doubled to account for the conjugate partner); separately, real event weights give $A_m(-\eta) = \mathrm{conj}(A_m(\eta))$, so per-event phases are evaluated only on non-negative modes and the full $A_m$ table is assembled by reflection and conjugation. Measured combined speed-up is ~2× across $r = 2, 3, 4$ in both modes, with agreement to machine precision (max relative error $\sim 10^{-15}$). Gated onto `_ma_per_attr_inner_matrix_rel` for $r = 2, 3, 4$: the grid path costs $K^2$ per event pair while the branch costs the $(r-1)$-dimensional mode grid, so the branch is taken while the grid stays under $1000 \cdot K^2 \cdot n_x \cdot n_y$, plus a hard $4 \times 10^6$-point memory cap. Calibrated against measured wall times over $\sigma/P \in [0.0025, 0.05]$, $K \in [4, 100]$, and event counts 1 to 30; without the gate the small-$\sigma/P$ corner regressed up to 12×. With it, measured gains run from ~1.3× to ~1400× and no measured cell regresses. Verified against the grid path at $\sim 3 \times 10^{-13}$ across $r = 2, 3, 4$ in both periodic and non-periodic modes (absolute scale included, $C_r$ derived analytically with no fitted parameters). The MATLAB twin is pending; until it lands the two languages agree in value but differ in route for relative-mode inner products. Verified in `tests/test_spectral_rel_ip.py`.

- **`tensorHarmonicity` batched mode rewritten to a dedup-and-batch path.** The previous per-row main loop has been replaced by a two-pass implementation: pass 1 computes per-row metadata (`nP`, `dup`, canonical chord key, interval vector) and identifies valid rows; pass 2 groups valid rows by `(nP, dup)`, deduplicates canonical chords within each group, and issues a single batched call to the Möbius relative-mode evaluator per group. Same FP path as scalar mode (so batched values match scalar values to machine precision; previously this held by the result cache, now it holds by construction). For typical uniform-cardinality batches the loop collapses to a single Möbius-method call. The previous warm-up + 10-row calibration estimate has been retired in favour of a row-count gate on the verbose-mode print (silent for fewer than 100 rows, matching the `min_print_sec=10` semantics of the other batched functions). The old `localPackTensorNV` / `_chord_canonical_key` plumbing is no longer needed in the rewrite path; the canonical key is computed directly.

### Changed — Lazy density-struct in `buildExpTens`

- **`buildExpTens` defaults to lazy density construction.** The `lazy` keyword (default `true`) controls whether the expensive density fields (`U_perm`, `wJ`, `V_comb`, `wV_comb`) are materialised at build time. v2.1 and earlier always materialised them; v2.2 builds a "skinny" density struct and defers the expensive fields until a consumer needs them. Most consumers (Möbius-method cosine, eval, entropy) do not need the fields and benefit from the deferral. Consumers that do need the fields (Bulger's method, the centres-array eval) call `ensureExpTensExpensive(dens)` (idempotent) before reading; this is wired internally throughout the toolbox. End-user behaviour is unchanged at the API level. A single existing test that asserted `r=1, isRel=true` errored at build time was relaxed to a warning (id `buildExpTens:isRelDegenerate`) since v2.2's well-defined treatment (constant 0-D space, total mass = $\sum w$, Rényi-2 = 0) makes the configuration valid rather than ill-formed.

### Changed — Console output for batched calls: dispatch announces, cost estimates, and adaptive countdowns

Several user-facing console-output behaviours unified across the five batched-input helpers (`cosSimExpTens`, `templateHarmonicity`, `spectralEntropy`, `virtualPitches`, `entropyExpTens`) so each long-running call emits a consistent, lightly-throttled trace: one dispatch announce per top-level user call, an empirical cost estimate when total runtime is expected to exceed ~10 s, and an adaptive row-count countdown when total runtime is expected to exceed ~5 s. All three pieces are present in both languages with matching semantics; verbose-gating (per-call) and `showHints`-gating (toolbox-wide) are decoupled in both languages. See USER_GUIDE.md §4 "Informational messages" for the user-facing description.

- **Dispatch-announce throttle scope: once-per-session → once per top-level user call.** Each top-level toolbox entry resets the dispatch seen-set on entry. New helpers `matlab/+internal/dispatchScope.m` and `python/mpt/_defaults.py:_dispatch_scope` (the latter a thread-local, depth-counted, re-entrant context manager) implement the scoping. Nested toolbox calls within a single top-level user call share a seen-set, so a batched-raw loop produces one announce per unique dispatch rather than one per item; successive top-level calls each re-announce. Public entry points wrap their bodies via `@_with_dispatch_scope` (Python) or the scope-acquire pattern (MATLAB). The previously session-scoped throttle on dispatch announces is gone; what remains session-scoped is only the one-time `showHints` performance hint (kernel-evaluation hint), which is still cleared only by `reset_defaults` / `mptDefaults('reset')`.

- **Dispatch-announce message format and throttle key: routing reason dropped from both.** Unprobed messages read `cosSimExpTens: chose 'bulger' path.`; probed messages read `cosSimExpTens: chose 'bulger' path (estimated X s); Ctrl+C to cancel.`. The throttle key is `(function, chosen)`, so two different routing reasons leading to the same chosen path within one top-level call collapse to a single announce. Dropping the reason from both the key and the message honours the principle that the visible distinction that matters is which path ran, not why.

- **Adaptive countdown.** Replaces the previous hardcoded "every 100 rows" cadence with an adaptive stride chosen from the empirical calibration. New helpers `matlab/+internal/progressStride.m` and `python/mpt/_utils.py:progress_stride` pick the smallest "nice" stride from `{1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000, 10000}` such that each print interval takes at least 5 s at the calibrated per-row cost (so 1 ms/row → stride 5000; 1 s/row → stride 5; 10 s/row → stride 1, i.e. print every row). Combined with a `showProgress = (estTotal >= 5)` gate set inside each helper's calibration block, this means: extrapolated total runtime under 5 s → silent regardless of row count; runtime over 5 s → progress at the adaptive stride plus a final flush. No more hardcoded 100-row floor. The per-iteration check is uniform across all five MATLAB and four Python batched helpers: `verbose && showProgress && (mod(k, progStride) == 0 || k == nRows)`. The unit reported is `unique pairs computed` for `cosSimExpTens` and `rows computed` for the others.

- **Demos updated.** `demo_genChainPcs.m`, `demo_gen_chain_pcs.py`, and `demo_edo_approx.py` now pass `verbose=true` to their long-running batched `cosSimExpTens` call so the estimate and countdown surface on the slow sweep, matching the MATLAB `demo_edoApprox.m` counterpart. `demo_triadConsonance.m` / `demo_triad_consonance.py` already used `verbose=true`; `demo_triadSpcsGrid.{m,py}` deliberately stay silent (consistent with their MATLAB counterpart).

- **Demo rename and relabelling: SPCS → PCS where there is no spectral enrichment.** `demo_genChainSpcs.m` is renamed to `demo_genChainPcs.m` (and its Python counterpart `demo_gen_chain_spcs.py` to `demo_gen_chain_pcs.py`), and all "SPCS" and "spectral pitch class similarity" labelling in both these demos and in `demo_edoApprox.m` / `demo_edo_approx.py` is corrected to "PCS" / "pitch class similarity". These demos compute cosine similarity directly on the pitch-class multisets, without `addSpectra` partials applied to either side — that is, **pitch class similarity (PCS)**, not the spectrally enriched form (SPCS). The mislabelling dated back to v2.0.0 when the demos were first added; the implementations were always PCS. (`demo_triadSpcsGrid.{m,py}` remains correctly labelled — it does apply `addSpectra` and so genuinely is SPCS.) USER_GUIDE.md §10 demo-table entries updated accordingly.

- **Associated cleanups.** (1) `entropyExpTens` / `entropy_exp_tens` Rényi-2 path now emits a dispatch announce (it was previously silent in both languages, an unintentional asymmetry with the Shannon path). (2) `tensorHarmonicity` no longer emits a wrapper-level announce; the inner `evalExpTens` does it (the wrapper announce was redundant and named the less informative function). (3) MATLAB test suite (`test_mpt.m`) now silences `showHints` at suite level to match Python's `python/tests/conftest.py` autouse fixture; tests that explicitly verify announce content (`test_dispatcher_ip_probe.m`, `test_tensor_harmonicity_orbit.m`, `test_harmony.m`) opt back in via local `mptDefaults('showHints', true)` save/restore. (5) Python dispatch tests (`test_dispatcher_ip_probe.py`, `test_dispatcher_probe.py`) updated for the new format and split the previous throttle test into within-call and across-call cases reflecting the new contract.

### Changed — `windowedSimilarity` argument order (breaking)

- **Positional argument order: `(densContext, densQuery, windowSpec, offsets, ...)`.** The first positional argument is the operand the window multiplies (the operand whose region is selected by each sweep position); the second is the unwindowed comparison template. This reflects the asymmetric roles: the context is what slides under the window; the query is the reference shape whose self inner product appears in the denominator under both normalisation modes. v2.1 took `(densQuery, densContext, ...)`; existing call sites must swap their first two positional arguments. The list-mode cartesian output shape follows the same convention: `(n_c, n_q, M)`, versus `(n_q, n_c, M)` in v2.1.

### Added — `normalize` NV on `cosSimExpTens` and `windowedSimilarity`

- **`normalize` / `normalise` keyword on both functions.** Two values, `'cosine'` and `'oneSidedDenom'`, case-insensitive on the value; both spellings of the keyword name are accepted. On `cosSimExpTens` / `cos_sim_exp_tens` the default is `'cosine'` — the strict shape-only cosine similarity $\langle X, Y \rangle / \sqrt{\langle X, X \rangle \langle Y, Y \rangle}$, bounded in $[-1, 1]$ and invariant to positive scalars on either operand. On `windowedSimilarity` / `windowed_similarity` the default is `'oneSidedDenom'` — the magnitude-aware $\langle h \cdot f_C, f_Q\rangle / \langle f_Q, f_Q\rangle$ (divide only by the query's unwindowed self inner product), giving self-similarity at full window coverage equal to 1 and an unbounded range above. The alternative on either function reaches across: `cosSimExpTens(..., 'normalize', 'oneSidedDenom')` divides by the second operand's self inner product alone (a magnitude-aware reading sensitive to scalar rescalings of the first operand), and `windowedSimilarity(..., 'normalize', 'cosine')` gives the strict shape-only cosine for the windowed comparison. The strict shape-only cosine on `windowedSimilarity` is closed-form only when the pointwise square $h^2$ of the window remains in the toolbox's `(size, mix)` family: pure Gaussian (`mix = 0`, with $h^2$ Gaussian and width scaled by $1/\sqrt 2$) and pure boxcar (`mix = 1`, with $h^2 = h$). Intermediate `mix` raises with a clean message directing the user to `'oneSidedDenom'` or to set the mix to 0 or 1. See User Guide §3.1 "Normalisation: one-sided versus strict shape-only".

### Changed — `windowedSimilarity` is the sole user-facing entry point for windowed similarity

- **`cosSimExpTens` / `cos_sim_exp_tens` no longer accept `WindowedMaetDensity` operands.** In v2.1, passing a `WindowedMaetDensity` as one operand silently dispatched to the closed-form windowed inner product, returning a magnitude-aware similarity (using the unwindowed L2 norms of both operands as the divisor) rather than a strict cosine similarity in $[0, 1]$. The function name `cosSimExpTens` promises the latter, so the dispatch was a semantic crack: a user calling `cosSimExpTens(densQ, wmd)` got an operation with substantially different mathematical content and value range than `cosSimExpTens(densQ, densC)`. v2.2 raises a clear error in both languages (`cosSimExpTens:windowedNotSupported` in MATLAB, `TypeError` in Python) directing users to `windowedSimilarity`, which is now the sole user-facing entry point for the windowed similarity in both the single-offset and multi-offset cases. The list-mode dispatcher rejects `WindowedMaetDensity` entries at the same point.

- **`windowedSimilarity` now emits the standard console-output pattern.** Once per top-level user call: a dispatch announce `windowedSimilarity: chose 'direct' path.` (the closed-form windowed inner product is the `direct` method — a single closed-form expression evaluated as written, with no clever decomposition such as Bulger's inclusion–exclusion or the orbit-collapsed Möbius reformulation). When the sweep is expected to take at least 10 s, a cost-estimate line `windowedSimilarity (batched, M rows): estimated time ~X s (Ctrl+C to cancel).` derived from the standard warm-up + 5-sample empirical calibration. When the sweep is expected to take at least 5 s, an adaptive countdown at the cadence picked by `internal.progressStride` / `_utils.progress_stride`, plus a final flush `windowedSimilarity: done.`. All five pieces (announce, warm-up, estimate, countdown, done flush) are gated on `verbose=true` and `showHints=true` in the same way as the other batched helpers.

### Performance

These entries cover inner-loop refinements to the kernel-evaluation paths in addition to the analytical-method gains documented above under *Added — Möbius method*.

- **`cosSimExpTens` periodic-relative IP kernel: redundant component-wise outer wrap eliminated.** In the periodic-relative case the IP kernel previously applied an outer per-component wrap of the difference tensor before computing the pairwise-wrap quadratic form $Q$. Pairwise wrapping subsumes outer wrapping when both `isRel` and `isPer` are true, so the outer wrap is redundant work. Removing it gives roughly 1.5–2× speedup on the cos-sim rel+per path in both languages. Applies symmetrically to the MAET log-kernel accumulator (`_ma_log_kernel` in Python; the corresponding helpers in `cosSimExpTens.m`), the chunked and full SA IP cores in `cosine.py`, and the windowed-IP path (`+internal/windowedInnerProduct.m` and its Python counterpart).

- **`templateHarmonicity` / `template_harmonicity` cross-correlation: `np.convolve` → `scipy.signal.correlate` (Python).** The chord-template cross-correlation at the heart of `templateHarmonicity` switches from `np.convolve(chord, tmpl[::-1])` to `scipy.signal.correlate(chord, tmpl, method='auto')`. Same numerical output; ~6× faster for the relevant template/chord sizes via SciPy's FFT-based correlation path at `method='auto'`'s crossover.

- **`windowedSimilarity` per-density self-IP denominators hoisted out of the offset sweep.** The constant-across-offsets self-inner-product factors $\langle T_a, T_a \rangle$ and $\langle T_b, T_b \rangle$ in the windowed-similarity normaliser were being recomputed inside the per-offset loop. They are now computed once per pair and reused across all offsets, via a new `_cached_norms` kwarg on the Python helper and corresponding norm-only-mode signature on the MATLAB helper. Speedups of ~2.6× on `demo_maetWindowing` and ~1.6× on `demo_windowingReference`.

- **`evalExpTens` / `eval_exp_tens` periodic-relative kernel inner loop.** The pairwise-wrap loop introduced by the Eq 6 correctness fix (see *Fixed* above) uses vectorised slot-0 wraps and an in-place inner pair loop with preallocated workspaces in Python (`np.subtract / np.divide / np.floor / np.multiply / np.add` with `out=` parameters). The hybrid pattern bounds the worst-case end-to-end slowdown vs the prior (incorrect) algebraic form at ~2× rather than the ~4× a naïve pairwise-wrap implementation would impose. MATLAB sites use the same hybrid pattern (slot-0 wraps vectorised, inner pairs looped). Applies to all SA and MA centres-path eval sites in both languages.

### Internal

- **`ensureExpTensExpensive` moved to `matlab/+internal/`.** The lazy-density materialiser is a toolbox-internal helper called only by consumer entry points (`cosSimExpTens`, `evalExpTens`, `windowedSimilarity`) on density inputs, and was the one top-level MATLAB function not part of the documented user-facing surface. Relocated to `matlab/+internal/ensureExpTensExpensive.m` to follow standard MATLAB +package conventions for non-user-facing helpers and to match the placement pattern of the other internal utilities (`gaussianKernelSum`, `chordCacheKey`, `dispatchScope`, etc.). Callers updated to `internal.ensureExpTensExpensive(...)`. No API or behaviour change for users; the function was never documented as user-callable. Python has the analogous `_ensure_exp_tens_expensive` already module-private (underscore prefix), so no Python-side change.

- **Closed-form windowed inner product extracted to an internal helper.** In v2.1 the closed-form windowed inner product was implemented inline inside `cosSimExpTens.m` / `cos_sim_exp_tens`; v2.2 moves it to `matlab/+internal/windowedInnerProduct.m` and `python/mpt/tensor._windowed_inner_product`, called from `windowedSimilarity`'s sweep loop. Not a public entry point — single windowed similarities at a chosen centre are obtained through `windowedSimilarity` with a single-column `offsets` (default `reference` is the query's centroid; pass `'reference'` / `reference=` explicitly to measure from an alternative origin).

- **Cross-language equivalence tests.** Hardcoded golden values pin down numerical agreement between the MATLAB and Python implementations to $10^{-8}$ relative across the v2.2 surface (Möbius-method cosine SA + MA including the safe/unsafe hybrid, Rényi-2 entropy SA + MA, Möbius-method `tensorHarmonicity`, and Möbius-method `evalExpTens`). See `tests/test_cross_language_golden.{m,py}`.

- **Cross-language speed parity for the Möbius method.** Targeted spot-checks at $(r, K) \in \{(2, 8), (3, 8), (3, 12), (4, 8), (5, 6)\}$ time the MATLAB Möbius-method IP/eval evaluators against their Python NumPy-einsum equivalents at matched inputs. The contraction graph is precomputed once per orbit at table-build time (see "Orbit-table contraction-graph precomputation" below) and consumed at runtime by `mobius.executeRecipe` (MATLAB) or `np.einsum(..., optimize=path)` (Python), avoiding per-call dispatch overhead in the contraction step. MATLAB / Python ratios are median 0.53× across the spot-check range (range 0.32×–1.37×, MATLAB generally the faster of the two at the median). The Möbius and Bulger methods remain mathematically equivalent at default settings; users do not need to choose between them. See `tests/bench_orbit_xlang.{m,py}` for the bench harness; `+mobius/buildContractRecipe.m` and `+mobius/executeRecipe.m` (MATLAB) and `_mobius._compute_einsum_path_*` (Python) for the implementations.

- **Orbit-table contraction-graph precomputation.** Each orbit table entry carries precomputed contraction-graph information embedded at build time:
  - **MATLAB** orbit structs carry three recipe fields (`recipeIP`, `recipeGrid`, `recipeBatched`) corresponding to the three IP-consumer call patterns (`mobius.innerProductOrbit`, `mobius.innerProductOrbitGrid`, `mobius.innerProductOrbitPwBatched`). Each recipe is a struct array of merge steps with precomputed `lhsPerm` / `rhsPerm` / dim-class counts. Runtime calls `mobius.executeRecipe`, which executes the recipe directly without invoking `intersect`, `setdiff`, `unique`, or `ismember`.
  - **Python** orbit entries carry three precomputed `np.einsum_path` outputs (`einsum_path`, `einsum_path_grid`, `einsum_path_pw_batched`) and one pre-built einsum string (`einsum_str_pw_batched`). Runtime calls `np.einsum(..., optimize=path)`, skipping NumPy's per-call path optimisation.

  Table loaders (`mobius.getOrbitTable` in MATLAB, `_mobius._ensure_paths` in Python) augment in-memory entries with these fields if absent from the loaded file, ensuring forward-compatibility with patch-version table additions.

- **`windowedSimilarity` propagates `truncation_sigmas` and `kernel_precision` deeply.** The kwargs are accepted at the `windowedSimilarity` boundary and threaded explicitly through the call chain: `_windowed_similarity_pair` (Python) and the corresponding MATLAB local function carry the kwargs, the inner cosine-similarity computation forwards them on every call, and recursive `windowedSimilarity` invocations propagate them onwards. Explicit threading is robust under recursion and thread-safe.

- **Dispatcher routing — analytical pre-screen + warmup probe.** When the analytical cost model doesn't clearly favour one path, the eval and inner-product dispatchers fall back to a wallclock probe that times each candidate path on a small subset of the workload. Each path runs a warmup pass (discarded) before its timed pass, so the measurement is from a fully-cache-warmed steady state and is independent of whichever path most recently executed on the full workload. The analytical pre-screen fires when one path's analytical cost is at least 3× smaller than the other (constants `PRESCREEN_CENTRES_DOMINANCE`, `PRESCREEN_ORBIT_DOMINANCE`, `PRESCREEN_IP_DOMINANCE` in both languages), skipping the probe entirely in the clear-winner case. With this design, identical workloads receive identical routing decisions on every call.

- **Vectorised multi-dimensional truncated kernel-sum path.** `localTruncatedKernelSum` in `matlab/+internal/gaussianKernelSum.m` processes (query, neighbour-bucket) pairs in vector form: for each chunk of queries it expands all $3^{\text{dim}}$ neighbour buckets in one matrix operation, masks out-of-bounds neighbours and empty buckets via vectorised comparisons, expands each (query, run-bucket) pair into per-centre pairs via cumsum-based ragged indexing, and accumulates kernel sums via `accumarray`. Memory budgeting matches the exact path's heuristic (per-chunk peak $\sim (\text{dim} + 1) \cdot n_J \cdot \text{chunkSize}$ scalars, capped at 50% of `MaxPossibleArrayBytes` or a 4 GB fallback). Applies whenever `truncationSigmas` is finite and the workload is multi-dimensional; the 1-D path is separately vectorised along sorted centres.

- **`kernelChunkBytes` / `kernel_chunk_bytes` toolbox default.** All kernel-matrix workloads (centres-path and IP-path evaluators in both languages, plus the Möbius relative-mode evaluator) share a single per-chunk byte budget configurable via this default. The factory value `'auto'` resolves at call time to half of currently available physical memory (Linux `/proc/meminfo` `MemAvailable`; macOS `vm_stat` `free + inactive + speculative`; Windows `memory().PhysicalMemory.Available` in MATLAB and `GlobalMemoryStatusEx` via `ctypes` in Python). A 4 GiB fallback applies if all platform queries fail. Explicit positive-integer byte values override globally via `mptDefaults('kernelChunkBytes', N)` (MATLAB) or `mpt.set_default(kernel_chunk_bytes=N)` (Python). Per-query allocation estimates account for the broadcast difference tensor, its square, and the summed-then-exponentiated intermediate being briefly co-resident (`(2 \cdot \text{dim} + 2) \cdot n_J \cdot \text{bytesPerScalar}` for the broadcast-pattern sites; site-specific formulas for the IP-side and Möbius-relative paths). Resolution happens on every chunker call, so the budget tracks memory pressure across a session. The platform queries are factored into `internal.availableMemory` (MATLAB) and `mpt._utils.available_memory_bytes` (Python); resolution into `internal.kernelChunkBytesResolved` and `mpt._utils.kernel_chunk_bytes_resolved`.

## [2.1.0] — 2026-05-08

### Overview

Version 2.1.0 generalises the expectation tensor framework to **multiple attributes per event** (multi-attribute expectation tensors, MAETs), and adds a **post-tensor windowing** mechanism for sliding-window template matching along any MAET group. Together these let the toolbox handle questions that the single-attribute treatment of v2.0.0 could not: locating motif recurrences over time, probe-tone scanning on irregularly timed contexts, polyphonic voice-aware analyses, and multi-quantity similarity (e.g., pitch combined with register, timbre, or metrical position) with the same closed-form analytical cosine-similarity inner product that underlies the single-attribute case.

Two cross-event preprocessing primitives, `differenceEvents` and `bindEvents`, support analyses based on inter-event differences and on n-tuples of consecutive events. Two utilities for sequential analysis, `continuity` and `seqWeights`, also accompany the main additions. A categorical-encoding utility, `simplexVertices`, supplies equidistant vertex coordinates for simplex-coded categorical attributes (voice, instrument, etc.) suitable for MAET inputs.

This release also adds **soft (`sigma > 0`) support to the structural measures `sameness` and `coherence`** — both gain an optional positive `sigma` argument that softens the discrete equality / ordering tests against Gaussian positional uncertainty — and **a `sigmaSpace` flag, shared across `sameness`, `coherence`, and `nTupleEntropy`** (whose `sigma` argument already existed in v2.0), controlling whether `sigma` is interpreted as positional uncertainty on each event (the new default, propagated through index sharing among derived intervals) or as independent uncertainty on each derived interval (matching the v2.0 numerical behaviour of `nTupleEntropy` at `sigma > 0`). A separate strand adds **Monte Carlo sigma support to the Argand-DFT family** via the new `dftCircularSimulate` function: `balanceCircular`, `evennessCircular`, and `projCentroid` accept an optional `sigma` argument, with closed-form analytical damping where the geometry permits it (`projCentroid`, since the projection is linear in $F(0)$) and Monte Carlo where it does not (`balanceCircular`, `evennessCircular`).

A third major strand is the **unification of dispatch and addition of consumer-level batching**. The Python entry points `eval_exp_tens`, `cos_sim_exp_tens`, and `entropy_exp_tens` are now polymorphic: each accepts a single density object, a list of densities, or raw arrays for either single-multiset or batched-multiset evaluation. The MATLAB equivalents are likewise extended where each language idiomatically permits. The harmony, entropy, and consonance wrappers (`tensorHarmonicity`, `templateHarmonicity`, `virtualPitches`, `spectralEntropy`) accept 2-D pitch matrices for batched evaluation. The DFT-equivariant family (`dftCircular`, `meanOffset`, `edges`, `projCentroid`, `circApm`), the structural-measure family (`coherence`, `sameness`, `nTupleEntropy`), and the Monte Carlo functions (`balanceCircular`, `evennessCircular`) all gain batched-input dispatch. Every batched path uses canonical-form deduplication to collapse symmetry-equivalent rows onto a single cached computation. The previous standalone entry points `cos_sim_exp_tens_raw`, `eval_exp_tens_raw`, `batch_cos_sim_exp_tens`, and `batchCosSimExpTens` are now deprecated shims forwarding to the unified entry points.

The release is additive: existing v2.0.0 single-attribute calling conventions for `buildExpTens`, `evalExpTens`, `cosSimExpTens`, and `entropyExpTens` are preserved unchanged. The new multi-attribute calling form is dispatched on input shape (a cell / list of per-attribute arrays for `p`) and is otherwise separate. The structural and Argand-DFT extensions are likewise additive at `sigma = 0`; a small documented numerical change to `nTupleEntropy` at `sigma > 0` is described in [MIGRATION.md](MIGRATION.md) and below.

### Added — Multi-attribute expectation tensors

- **MAET calling form for `buildExpTens`, `evalExpTens`, `cosSimExpTens`, `entropyExpTens`.** Each now accepts a multi-attribute density specification alongside the v2.0.0 single-attribute form. A MAET carries $A$ attributes in $G \le A$ groups; each attribute has its own tuple order $r_a$ and per-event slot count $K_a$, and each group has shared $\sigma$, `isRel`, `isPer`, and `period`. The density factorises as a product of per-attribute Gaussian-mixture kernels, and the cosine-similarity inner product factorises analytically in the same way. The single-attribute case is the special case $A = 1$. See User Guide §3.1 for the mathematical formulation and the post-tensor windowing mechanism built on top of it.

### Added — Cross-event preprocessing

- **`differenceEvents`.** Preprocessing helper that replaces each selected group's event sequence with inter-event differences before tensor construction. Per-group differencing order $k$ produces the $k$-th finite differences along the event axis (reducing the event count by $k$); periods supplied per group wrap each raw difference to the shortest signed arc. Weights propagate as rolling products of width $k + 1$, interpretable as the probability that all constituents of a difference are jointly perceived. Inputs are restricted to $K_a = 1$ slots per event; see User Guide §3.1 (Preprocessing) for the rationale and the voices-as-attributes pipeline for polyphonic step-size analyses. Output feeds directly into `buildExpTens`. This supports analyses over interval content, IOIs, and higher-order differences without needing a dedicated interval representation.

- **`bindEvents`.** Preprocessing helper that gathers $n$ consecutive events into a single super-event with $n$ separate attributes carrying the values at lags $0$ through $n-1$. Output is a length-$n$ list of $K_a \times N'$ matrices, suitable as the `pAttr` argument of `buildExpTens` with all $n$ attributes assigned to a single group sharing $\sigma$ and the other group-level parameters. Lag slots are kept as separate attributes (rather than a single $K_a = n$ multi-slot attribute) because lag identity is non-exchangeable — within-attribute multiset symmetry would otherwise collapse ordered tuples to unordered ones. Per-event weights propagate per-attribute (each output attribute inherits the slot weights of the underlying event at its lag); `buildExpTens` multiplies across attributes during tuple enumeration, so the end-to-end effective weight of a bound super-event equals the product of the $n$ constituent events' weights, matching the differencing rule. Inputs may have any $K_a \ge 1$: when the input attribute has multi-value slots, each output attribute carries the full $K_a$-slot vector of its underlying event, with within-attribute exchangeability preserved per output attribute. Default output has $N - n + 1$ super-events; a `circular` option produces $N$ by wrapping around the input sequence. Composes with `differenceEvents`: the standard pipeline `differenceEvents` $\to$ `bindEvents` $\to$ `buildExpTens` $\to$ `entropyExpTens` recovers the n-tuple entropy of Milne & Dean (2016) at $\sigma \to 0$ and uniform weights, and extends it to the smoothed continuous case, weighted events, and non-periodic domains; for $n \ge 2$ the bound MAET is itself an $n$-dimensional density supporting cosine-similarity comparison of n-tuple distributions across pieces and the rest of the MAET pipeline. Used without preceding differencing, produces n-grams in absolute pitch or time register, useful when register or absolute timing carries musical information that the differenced view discards.

### Added — Post-tensor windowing

- **`windowTensor`.** Wraps a MAET with a per-group window specification (widths, shapes, and centres), returning a `WindowedMaetDensity` that is consumed lazily by `evalExpTens` (pointwise multiplication by the window) and by `cosSimExpTens` (closed-form windowed inner product). Windows are specified in a one-parameter family by `size` (width, in multiples of group $\sigma$) and `mix` in $[0, 1]$ (shape: pure Gaussian at 0, pure rectangular at 1, rectangular-convolved-with-Gaussian in between). A numeric size-1 input to `window_spec['centre']` (Python: a Python scalar, 0-D ndarray, or numeric ndarray of size 1; MATLAB: a numeric scalar or 1×1 numeric array) is broadcast to fill every per-attribute slot uniformly; convenient when one window centre value is wanted everywhere. List/cell inputs are always interpreted structurally and never broadcast.

- **`windowedSimilarity`.** Sweeps a series of window centres across a windowed context and returns the windowed-similarity profile against a query. Implements cross-correlation: at each sweep centre $c$ in a windowed group, the query is translated so that its effective-space mean within that group moves onto $c$, so a peak at $c$ means the query pattern is present in the context near $c$. The normaliser is one-sided by the query's unwindowed $L^2$ self inner product, $s = \langle h \cdot f_X, f_Y \rangle / \langle f_Y, f_Y \rangle$, making the returned profile magnitude-aware (denser matching content gives a higher value than sparser content). The output is *not* a cosine similarity in the strict sense (the unwindowed denominator means it is not bounded in $[-1, 1]$ across sweep positions); "cosine similarity" is reserved for the strict shape-only form, which is not currently implemented in the toolbox. See User Guide §3.1 "Post-tensor windowing".

- **Polymorphic query and context.** Either or both of `densQuery` and `densContext` may be a cell array (MATLAB) or list (Python) of MaetDensity structs, in which case the function returns a cell array (MATLAB) or list (Python) of profiles. A new `'mode'` name-value option controls the pairing: `'pairwise'` (require equal lengths; pair element-by-element; returns a 1-by-n cell), `'cartesian'` (cross every query with every context; returns an n_q-by-n_c cell), or `'auto'` (default; pairwise when lengths match, cartesian otherwise). Option II shape rule: a length-1 list returns a length-1 cell. Scalar inputs continue to dispatch to the existing single-pair path with byte-identical results. The `'reference'` option also gains a per-query form: a length-n_q cell-of-cells (each entry itself a 1-by-A cell of per-attribute reference vectors), disambiguated from the shared length-A form by element type.

- **`reference` option.** A `reference` keyword argument (Python) / name-value pair (MATLAB) that allows the offset-axis origin per attribute to be set explicitly. When absent or `None` / `[]` (default), the prior unweighted-centroid behaviour is preserved byte-for-byte. When supplied, the call uses the given reference as the offset-axis origin per attribute — for example, calibrated to a canonical harmonic query so that offsets retain a stable musical-interval reading across queries with different slot counts, slot values, or slot weights. See User Guide §3.1 "Post-tensor windowing" for the practical guidance on default versus fixed-reference choices and the `demo_windowingReference` / `demo_windowing_reference` demo for a worked walkthrough.

- **Wrapped-Gaussian image summation for periodic groups.** For periodic groups, `windowedSimilarity` / `windowed_similarity` and the windowed branch of `evalExpTens` / `eval_exp_tens` use the wrapped Gaussian window (or the wrapped rectangular-convolved-with-Gaussian for `mix > 0`): the sum of line-case window functions at all periodic images of the centre, $h_{\text{per}}(x) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} h_{\text{line}}(x - nP)$. The windowed inner product inherits this linearity and is computed as a sum of line-case inner products evaluated at shifted centres, truncated adaptively when the latest image-pair's contribution falls below $10^{-12}$ of the running maximum (or $10^{-7}$ for `kernel_precision = 'single'`) — the result equals the exact wrapped-window inner product to floating-point precision across all regimes of $\lambda\sigma / P$. For 1-D groups and multi-D absolute groups, the per-pair factor factorises across axes and the image sum factorises into per-axis sums (cost linear in dimension); for multi-D relative groups the line-case formula is used directly (image summation for the non-factorising kernel is a future extension). See User Guide §3.1 "Post-tensor windowing".

- **Analytical closed form.** The cosine-similarity inner product between a windowed MAET and an unwindowed MAET is closed-form analytical across the full `(size, mix)` family for all 1-D groups and all multi-dimensional absolute groups; for multi-dimensional relative groups, it is established for the pure-Gaussian window only, with approximate handling of the full family left as a future extension.

### Added — Sequential-analysis utilities

- **`continuity`.** Expected length and signed magnitude of the backward same-direction run leading up to each query, under Gaussian pitch uncertainty. Smoothed directional-agreement score $a_k = \mathrm{erf}(i_k/(2\sigma)) \cdot \mathrm{erf}(i_N/(2\sigma))$ drives a backward walk from the most recent context interval. Two break-threshold modes (`'strict'` with $\theta = 0$, `'lenient'` with $\theta = -1$) and an explicit-threshold override. Returns `(count, magnitude)`; `magnitude / count` is a trend-slope measure. An optional `w` argument supplies per-event salience weights; the salience of difference event $k$ is the rolling product $w_k \cdot w_{k+1}$, matching `differenceEvents` at order 1, and scales each interval's contribution to `count` and `magnitude`. Internally, the function routes through `differenceEvents` to compute both the context intervals and their weights, so the preprocessing is shared with the MAET-with-differencing pipeline; the read-out (a directional, order-preserving backward walk with a break condition) is not. Defined only on linearly ordered domains.

- **`seqWeights`.** Constructs length-$N$ position-weight vectors from a named specification (`'flat'`, `'primacy'`, `'recency'`, `'exponentialFromStart'`, `'exponentialFromEnd'`, `'uShape'`, `'uAsym'`) or an explicit profile. A *U-shape* profile is one with high weight at both the start and end of the sequence and lower weight in the middle, modelling the combined primacy and recency effects in serial memory (recent items remembered well, oldest items remembered well, items in the middle remembered worst). Supports time-based decay via an optional time index. Combines an intrinsic per-event salience with a memory-decay or attentional profile in a single inspectable vector, usable anywhere a weight argument is accepted. The `'uAsym'` profile is a three-parameter U-shape with separate decay rates for the primacy and recency components: `alpha * exp(-decayRateStart * t) + (1 - alpha) * exp(-decayRateEnd * (t_end - t))`; reduces to `'uShape'` when `decayRateStart == decayRateEnd`. In addition to a named string and an explicit length-$N$ vector, `spec` may be a function (Python callable, MATLAB function handle) of the time vector that returns a length-$N$ profile, allowing arbitrary user-defined profiles (hyperbolic decay, custom mixtures, etc.) to be applied with the same length validation and `w` multiplication as built-in specs.

### Added — Categorical-encoding utility

- **`simplexVertices`** (MATLAB), **`simplex_vertices`** (Python). Returns the $N$ vertices of a regular $(N-1)$-simplex centred at the origin in $\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$, with all pairwise vertex distances equal to a specified `edgeLength` (default 1). Supports the simplex-coded encoding of an $N$-level categorical attribute (voice identity, instrument, etc.) for MAET inputs: each level is represented by an $(N-1)$-dimensional coordinate row, fed as values for the $N-1$ numerical sub-attributes of a single categorical group. All levels are pairwise equidistant, so no level is privileged over any other — in contrast to dummy or treatment coding. Construction uses the centred standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^N$ projected onto an orthonormal basis of $\mathbf{1}^\perp$; the result is rotation-equivalent across choices of basis, which is irrelevant for downstream MAET computations.

### Added — Data structures

- **`MaetDensity`** and **`WindowedMaetDensity`.** Tagged dataclasses (Python) / tagged structs (MATLAB) produced by `buildExpTens` (multi-attribute form) and `windowTensor` respectively. `evalExpTens`, `cosSimExpTens`, and `entropyExpTens` dispatch on the tag.

### Added — Soft (`sigma > 0`) structural measures

- **`sameness` / `coherence` — `sigma` argument with `sigmaSpace` flag.** Both functions now accept an optional positive `sigma` that softens the discrete equality / ordering tests against Gaussian positional uncertainty. With `sigma = 0` (default) the v2.0 hard counts are recovered byte-for-byte. With `sigma > 0`, `sameness` replaces each `[d1 = d2]` indicator with a Gaussian match kernel $\exp(-(d_2 - d_1)^2 / (2V))$, and `coherence` replaces each `[d2 \le d1]` indicator with the Gaussian-CDF probability $\Phi((d_1 - d_2)/\sqrt{V})$. The variance $V$ depends on the new `sigmaSpace` name-value flag: with `sigmaSpace = 'position'` (default), $V$ is computed per pair of intervals from the sharing structure of their endpoints (so the diatonic tritone, where the fourth and fifth share both endpoints with reinforcing signs, has $V = 8\sigma^2$ and the ordering test sees a tie at every $\sigma > 0$); with `sigmaSpace = 'interval'`, $V = 2\sigma^2$ uniformly (intervals treated as independent draws, ignoring index correlations). Float positions and float `period` are accepted when `sigma > 0`. See User Guide §3.5 (Soft sigma semantics) and §6.5.

- **`nTupleEntropy` — `sigmaSpace` flag.** Adds a `sigmaSpace` name-value pair with the same semantics as `sameness` and `coherence`, governing whether the supplied `sigma` is treated as positional uncertainty on each event (the new default, with derived steps inheriting variance $2\sigma^2$ per slot via the marginal-matched approximation) or as independent uncertainty on each derived step (matching v2.0 numerical behaviour). At $n = 1$ the two flags are exactly related: `'position'` with $\sigma$ produces identical entropy to `'interval'` with $\sigma\sqrt{2}$. At $n \ge 2$ with `sigmaSpace = 'position'`, the current implementation uses the marginal-matched approximation (slots independent at $\sigma_{\text{eff}} = \sigma\sqrt{2}$); a one-time `UserWarning` (Python warning class `UserWarning` with message containing `marginal-matched`; MATLAB warning identifier `nTupleEntropy:positionApprox`) flags this. Full position-aware $n \ge 2$ support, requiring a non-diagonal Gaussian-mixture entropy routine, is planned for a future release. See User Guide §3.5 and §6.5.

- **`positionVariance`.** Shared helper that computes the variance of a signed sum of independently jittered positions, $V = \sigma^2 \sum_a (\sum \text{signs at index } a)^2$. Used internally by the position-aware paths of `sameness` and `coherence`; documented and exported because users implementing their own position-aware structural measures may want to call it directly. The same logic is exposed in Python as `mpt._utils.position_variance`.

### Added — Argand-DFT Monte Carlo

- **`dftCircularSimulate` / `dft_circular_simulate`.** Monte Carlo estimator of the distribution of Argand-DFT coefficient magnitudes under independent Gaussian positional jitter $\widetilde{p}_k = (p_k + \eta_k) \bmod P$, $\eta_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$, where $P$ is the period. For each draw, the perturbed positions are sorted (capturing the perceptual reordering when noise is comparable to the smallest event-to-event gap), the Argand vector is formed, and the DFT is computed. Returns the per-coefficient mean and standard deviation of $|\widetilde{F}(k)|$ across draws by default; a third optional output (`samples` / `return_samples=True`) yields the full $n_{\text{draws}} \times K$ magnitude matrix for histogram or quantile analysis. Name-value parameters `nDraws` (default 10000) and `rngSeed` for reproducibility.

- **`balanceCircular`, `evennessCircular` — `sigma` argument.** Both functions now accept an optional positive `sigma` that triggers Monte Carlo estimation via `dftCircularSimulate` and returns the *expected* balance / evenness under positional jitter. At `sigma = 0` the v2.0 deterministic value is recovered exactly. The MATLAB versions also return the per-coefficient standard deviation as an optional second left-hand-side output (idiomatic `nargout`-driven multi-return); the Python versions accept a `return_std=True` flag that switches the return type from scalar to `(mean, std)` tuple. Backward-compatible at `sigma = 0` (scalar return preserved). Name-value `nDraws` and `rngSeed` are forwarded to the simulator. The augmented triad — perfectly balanced at `sigma = 0` — exhibits the expected Rayleigh-bias positive shift of $E[|F(0)|]$ at `sigma > 0`. See User Guide §6.4.

- **`projCentroid` — `sigma` argument (analytical closed form).** Because $y(x) = \mathrm{Re}(F(0) \cdot e^{-2\pi i x / P})$ is linear in $F(0)$ and $F(0)$ is permutation-invariant under positional jitter, no Monte Carlo is needed for the mean projection: $E[y(x)] = \alpha_1 \cdot y_{\text{deterministic}}(x)$ where $\alpha_1 = \exp(-2\pi^2 \sigma^2 / P^2)$ is the standard kernel-smoothing damping factor. Phase is preserved exactly in expectation, so `centPhase` is unchanged; `centMag` returns $\alpha_1 \cdot |F(0)| = |E[\widetilde{F}(0)]|$, the magnitude of the *complex mean centroid* — consistent with the projection. The distinct scalar $E[|\widetilde{F}(0)|]$ — the *mean centroid magnitude under jitter*, which picks up positive Rayleigh-style bias — is what `balanceCircular(p, w, period, sigma)` returns; the two answer different balance-related questions (see User Guide §3.5 "Two scalars, two balance-related questions").

### Added — Unified dispatch and consumer-level batching

A coherent strand of changes makes the consumer-facing entry points polymorphic on input shape and adds 2-D-pitch-matrix batching to most function families. The convention throughout: each row of a 2-D pitch matrix is one multiset, the $M$ rows form the batch, columns are slots, and NaN-padding allows variable-cardinality batches; weight matrices match the pitch-matrix shape. Every batched path uses canonical-form deduplication — symmetry-equivalent rows (permutation of slots, transposition modulo the period in `isRel = true` modes, period-equivalence in `isPer = true` modes) are computed once per equivalence class and the result is mapped back to all matching rows. Which symmetries are exploited depends on what each function's output is actually invariant to (transposition-equivariant measures collapse only permutation and period-equivalence; transposition-invariant measures collapse all three). Name-value arguments such as `'spectrum'`, `'precision'`, and `'dedup'` are uniformly available across raw scalar and raw batched modes where they are meaningful. See User Guide §4 ("Consumer-level batching" and "Canonical-form deduplication") for the full convention.

- **`cosSimExpTens` / `cos_sim_exp_tens` — unified dispatch.** The function now accepts five input forms via type dispatch on its first argument: a single density struct (existing scalar SA / MA path), raw arguments for a single multiset (existing scalar SA / MA path), a cell array (MATLAB) or list (Python) of density structs (new "list mode" — returns a same-length cell / list of per-pair similarities), a 2-D pitch matrix with both dimensions > 1 (new "batched-raw mode" — rows are paired multisets, returns a length-`nRows` vector of similarities), and the existing MA cell-of-arrays raw form (unchanged). The Option II shape rule applies to list mode: a length-1 list returns a length-1 cell / list, never a scalar. Backward compatibility is preserved exactly: every v2.0 calling form produces byte-identical results. Row-vector and column-vector inputs continue to dispatch to the existing scalar SA raw path. The list and batched-raw modes are additive and mutually exclusive with the existing scalar / MA dispatches based on input shape.

- **`evalExpTens` / `eval_exp_tens` — unified dispatch.** Five input forms via type dispatch on the first argument: a single density struct, raw arguments for a single multiset, a cell / list of density structs (returns same-length cell / list of per-density value vectors), a 2-D pitch matrix (rows are multisets, returns an `nRows`-by-`nQ` matrix of values; NaN-padded rows handled identically to `batchCosSimExpTens`), and the existing MA cell-of-arrays raw form. Option II shape rule applies in list mode. Backward compatibility is preserved exactly. In list mode, the `X` argument is broadcast to all densities by default; passing a length-`n` cell of per-density query matrices treats each entry as the `X` for the corresponding density (disambiguated by element type for SA densities).

- **`entropyExpTens` / `entropy_exp_tens` — unified dispatch.** Symmetric additions to `cosSimExpTens` and `evalExpTens`: list mode (cell / list of density structs returns cell / list of entropies, Option II shape rule) and batched-raw mode (2-D pitch matrix returns `nRows`-by-1 vector of entropies; NaN-padded rows handled; rows with fewer than `r` valid pitches return `NaN`). Name-value arguments (`'normalize'`, `'base'`, `'nPointsPerDim'`, `'xMin'`, `'xMax'`, `'gridLimit'`) are forwarded to per-row computations.

- **List-mode broadcasting.** Either operand of `cosSimExpTens` may be a single density struct paired with a cell array (MATLAB) or list (Python) of density structs; the single struct is broadcast against every entry of the cell / list, returning a 1-by-`n` cell / array of similarities (Option II shape rule preserved: a length-1 cell returns a length-1 cell). The pairwise and length-mismatch error paths are unchanged. The `'mode'` `'cartesian'` shape supported by Python `cos_sim_exp_tens` for full list × list cross-products is *not* part of MATLAB list mode; MATLAB list mode remains pairwise-only (with scalar broadcast as the single new shape).

- **Batched-raw broadcasting.** When one of `P1`, `P2` is an `M`-by-`K` matrix (`M > 1`) and the other is a vector of length `K` (1-D, `1`-by-`K` row, or `K`-by-`1` column in MATLAB; 1-D or `(1, K)` 2-D in Python), the vector is broadcast across the matrix's `M` rows in NumPy / MATLAB implicit-expansion style, and the corresponding weights argument is broadcast in lockstep when non-empty. Eliminates the `repmat(refPitches, M, 1)` / `np.tile(ref_pitches, (M, 1))` idiom for the common "compare one reference multiset against many candidates" use case. Mismatched row counts (when neither operand is broadcastable) raise `cosSimExpTens:rowMismatch` (MATLAB) or `ValueError` (Python) with a clear message. Backward compatibility is preserved: explicitly tiled `M`-by-`K` inputs continue to return byte-identical results.

- **`tensorHarmonicity`, `templateHarmonicity`, `virtualPitches` — batched-input dispatch.** Each function now accepts `p` as a 2-D `nRows`-by-`K` matrix with both dimensions > 1, treating each row as a separate multiset and returning per-row results. `tensorHarmonicity` returns an `nRows`-by-1 column vector of harmonicities. `templateHarmonicity` returns `hMax` and `hEntropy` each as an `nRows`-by-1 column vector. `virtualPitches` returns `vp_p` and `vp_w` each as a 1-by-`nRows` cell array of column vectors — cell-of-arrays output is used because the cross-correlation profile length varies per row (depending on the chord's pitch range plus margin), so a numeric matrix alignment is not natural. NaN-padded rows are accepted (NaN entries dropped per row, consistent with `batchCosSimExpTens`); rows with fewer than the minimum required valid pitches yield `NaN` (or empty cell entries for `virtualPitches`). The scalar (vector input) path is unchanged and produces byte-identical results to v2.0. The `p (:,1)` argument-block validation has been relaxed to `{mustBeNumeric}` to admit matrices; row vectors and column vectors continue to dispatch to the scalar path. The `template_harmonicity_formulation_review.md` companion document records known limitations of the `templateHarmonicity` measures (interval-multiset symmetry of chord inversions; single-pitch / H1 hijacking at high spectrum-weight exponents; high-harmonic-density hijacking at low ρ with large N) which apply equally to scalar and batched calls.

- **`spectralEntropy` / `spectral_entropy` — 2-D batched dispatch.** Argument shape relaxed to accept a 2-D `(M, K)` pitch matrix in addition to the original 1-D form. Rows are chords; the return is a length-`M` column vector of per-row entropies. NaN-padded rows are accepted (rows with no valid pitches return `NaN`). Per-row dedup uses the v2.1 canonical-form key with `r=1`, `is_rel=true` (the function transposes internally), `is_per=false` — symmetry-equivalent inputs (transposed copies, permutations) share one cached entropy. Numerical results in scalar mode are unchanged.

- **Tier-1 batched dispatch (DFT-equivariant family): `dftCircular`, `meanOffset`, `edges`, `projCentroid`, `circApm`.** All five circular functions now accept a 2-D `(M, K)` pitch matrix in addition to their original 1-D form, and return 1-by-`M` cell arrays (MATLAB) / length-`M` lists of arrays (Python) for each output. NaN-padded rows are accepted; rows with no valid pitches give empty cell entries. Per-row dedup uses a canonical key built from sorted modular pitches plus matching weights, collapsing **permutation** and **period-equivalent** inputs onto one cached set of outputs. Dedup over **transposition is *not* applied** in this release — the DFT family is transposition-equivariant rather than invariant, and the per-output post-transforms required for full transposition dedup are non-trivial (especially for the query-grid functions whose outputs depend on user-supplied `x`); kept as a future optimisation. Weights may be `None`, the same shape as `P`, or a length-`K` vector broadcast across rows. For `circApm`, each `R` output is dense `period × period`, so memory scales as `M × period^2`; the docstring flags this and recommends chunked processing for large batches.

- **Tier-2 batched dispatch (structural functions): `coherence`, `sameness`, `nTupleEntropy`.** All three set-based circular functions now accept a 2-D `(M, K)` pitch matrix and return per-row results: length-`M` numeric vectors for scalar outputs (MATLAB column vectors / Python ndarrays), plus a length-`M` cell / list for `nTupleEntropy`'s variable-shape `tuples` output. NaN-padded rows are accepted; rows with no valid pitches give `NaN` in the scalar outputs and empty entries in the cell / list. These functions take no `w` argument (they're set-based and reject duplicates), so the batched signature has no weight handling. Per-row dedup keys differ across the three to match what each function's outputs are actually invariant to: `coherence` and `sameness` use the **necklace canonical form** of the cyclic adjacent intervals — collapsing permutation, period, and **transposition** symmetries onto a single cached result, since both functions are fully transposition-invariant on the circle in both `'position'` and `'interval'` modes. `nTupleEntropy` uses sorted-modular only (permutation + period); its `H` value is transposition-invariant but its `tuples` output reflects the input's cyclic ordering from sort-min and so differs across transposed inputs, which would break the dedup contract.

- **Tier-4 batched dispatch (Monte-Carlo functions): `balanceCircular`, `evennessCircular`.** Both functions now accept a 2-D `(M, K)` pitch matrix and return per-row `nRows`-by-1 column vectors (MATLAB) / length-`M` ndarrays (Python), with optional second output for per-row standard deviations. NaN-padded rows are accepted; rows with no valid pitches give `NaN` entries. New name-value pair `rngScope` (default `'canonical'`) controls how the per-row RNG seed is derived from the base `rngSeed` when `sigma > 0`: `'canonical'` derives each row's seed from the canonical-form key via FNV-1a hash, so canonical-form-equivalent rows share an identical Monte Carlo realisation and benefit from caching; `'row'` derives each row's seed from the row index, giving independent reproducible realisations per row and disabling caching. When `rngSeed` is empty, a session-random base seed is generated once per batched call so within-call dedup remains reproducible while across-call results differ. Per-row dedup uses sorted-modular canonical key (permutation + period). The same FNV-1a hash function is implemented identically in MATLAB and Python so a given canonical key produces the same derived seed in both languages.

### Changed

- **Python `mpt` package version bumped to `2.1.0`** (`__version__` and `pyproject.toml`).

- **`nTupleEntropy` / `n_tuple_entropy` refactored as a thin wrapper.** Internally now calls the `differenceEvents` $\to$ `bindEvents` $\to$ `buildExpTens` $\to$ `entropyExpTens` pipeline. With default arguments (`sigma = 0`, `nPointsPerDim = period`, `normalize = true`) it exactly replicates the discrete n-tuple entropy of Milne & Dean (2016) byte-for-byte; existing call sites at `sigma = 0` are unaffected. New optional name-value pairs expose the underlying flexibility: `nPointsPerDim` controls the entropy-evaluation grid resolution per dimension (the integer-step grid by default, finer for better resolution of smoothed densities). For non-integer values, weighted events, non-periodic domains, or to obtain the n-tuple density itself for similarity comparison and other MAET operations, call the primitives directly. **Numerical change at `sigma > 0`.** As described above under *Added — Soft (`sigma > 0`) structural measures*, the new `sigmaSpace` flag changes the default semantics of `sigma` at `sigma > 0`: v2.1.0's default `sigmaSpace = 'position'` treats `sigma` as positional uncertainty on each event, with derived steps inheriting variance $2\sigma^2$ per slot. v2.0.0's effective semantics treated `sigma` as independent per-step uncertainty, equivalent to `sigmaSpace = 'interval'`. The two coincide at `sigma = 0`. To recover v2.0.0 numerical results at `sigma > 0`, pass `sigmaSpace = 'interval'`. See [MIGRATION.md](MIGRATION.md) for a worked example.

- **`bindEvents` / `bind_events` documentation.** Added a clarification to the `circular` argument: it describes the *event sequence* (whether the last event connects back to the first), and is independent of the *positional periodicity* set in `buildExpTens` via its `isPer` / `period` arguments. Both combinations are meaningful: a non-circular sequence on a periodic domain (a non-cyclic motif living in pitch-class space), and a circular sequence on a linear domain (a cyclic rhythm represented in linear time, e.g., for windowed analysis). The two flags are orthogonal. No behavioural change — clarification only.

- **`estimateCompTime` / `estimate_comp_time` — new `minPrintSec` parameter, defaulting to `10` s; `Ctrl+C` and print thresholds collapsed.** The function gains a fifth optional argument (`minPrintSec` in MATLAB / `min_print_sec` in Python). Estimates below the threshold are silent even when `verbose` is true; the default of 10 s is chosen so that sub-10 s waits are their own diagnostic and only genuinely-actionable workloads earn screen real estate. The previous separate "`Ctrl+C to cancel` suffix above 2 s" threshold has been collapsed into the same parameter — every printed estimate now carries the cancellation reminder by construction. The change is uniform across all callers (`evalExpTens`, `cosSimExpTens`, `spectralEntropy`, `templateHarmonicity`, `virtualPitches`, and any user code that calls these in tight loops); no caller needs to opt in. Pass `0` to recover the old always-print behaviour, or set higher to surface only the slowest cases. Existing `verbose=false` callers retain full silence (unchanged).

- **New utility: `printBatchedEstimate` (MATLAB) / `maybe_print_batched_estimate` (Python).** Centralises the batched-mode upfront empirical-calibration print used by `templateHarmonicity`, `virtualPitches`, `spectralEntropy`, `entropyExpTens`, and `tensorHarmonicity` batched dispatch helpers. Threshold and formatting are kept consistent with `estimateCompTime`'s scalar print path: 10-s default `minPrintSec` (or `min_print_sec`), every printed estimate carries `(Ctrl+C to cancel)`, and time-formatting uses the same units.

- **`templateHarmonicity` / `template_harmonicity` — `verbose` option and time estimates.** Both languages gain a `verbose` name-value argument (MATLAB) / keyword argument (Python), default `true`, controlling whether the function prints an upfront time estimate — matching the behaviour `evalExpTens` and `cosSimExpTens` already had. Two estimation strategies, one per dispatch shape: scalar mode uses the kernel-only `estimateCompTime` / `estimate_comp_time` micro-benchmark; batched mode uses an empirical calibration that times a small sample of rows and extrapolates. The estimate is suppressed by `verbose=false`. Numerical results are unchanged.

- **`tensorHarmonicity` / `tensor_harmonicity` and `virtualPitches` / `virtual_pitches` — `verbose` option and time estimates.** Both functions gain a `verbose` name-value argument (MATLAB) / keyword argument (Python), default `true`, mirroring the `templateHarmonicity` addition. `tensorHarmonicity` scalar-mode forwards `verbose` to its inner `buildExpTens` call (the dominant cost for tensor harmonicity is template construction, not template evaluation); `virtualPitches` scalar-mode prints a kernel-only `estimateCompTime` estimate covering its two `evalExpTens` workloads. Both batched-mode helpers use empirical calibration. The estimate is suppressed by `verbose=false`. Numerical results are unchanged.

- **`spectralEntropy` / `spectral_entropy` — `verbose` option.** A `verbose` argument (default `true`) controls printing: scalar mode prints a kernel-only `estimateCompTime` estimate (sum of the dominant `evalExpTens` workload, dim=1); batched mode prints an empirical-calibration estimate with warm-up, in the same idiom as `templateHarmonicity` batched.

- **`entropyExpTens` / `entropy_exp_tens` — `verbose` option for SA batched dispatch.** A `verbose` name-value argument (MATLAB) / keyword argument (Python), default `true`, is added to the existing raw SA batched dispatch. When enabled and `M > 1`, the function prints an empirical-calibration upfront estimate. `verbose=false` silences.

- **Internal performance improvement for `r = 1` expectation tensors.** `buildExpTens` (MATLAB) and `build_exp_tens` (Python) now collapse equal-valued slots at build time when `r = 1`, in both the single-attribute legacy path and the multi-attribute (MAET) path. In the MAET case the collapse is applied per-attribute independently — only attributes with `r_a = 1` are collapsed; attributes with `r_a >= 2` in the same event are unaffected. Mathematically exact: the density at `r = 1` depends on the source multiset only through its measure on the value axis, so events with the same value contribute additively to the same Gaussian kernel and can be merged into a single source point with the sum of the original weights. No behavioural change, no API change. Speedup is proportional to the degree of repetition in the input; for probe-tone-style contexts (e.g., where 7 unique context pitch classes are repeated 3 times) the effective speedup on downstream `evalExpTens` and `cosSimExpTens` calls is approximately 9×. Not applied for `r >= 2`: at higher tuple orders the source multiplicity carries information about within-tuple structure (e.g., the `r = 2` relative density at interval 0 receives genuine contributions from pairs of equal-pitched events) and collapsing would alter the density.

### Design notes

**Additivity.** The v2.0.0 single-attribute calling conventions are preserved verbatim: `buildExpTens(p, w, sigma, r, isRel, isPer, period)`, `cosSimExpTens(p1, w1, p2, w2, sigma, r, isRel, isPer, period)`, and friends continue to work unchanged. The multi-attribute calling form is distinguished by passing `p` as a cell array (MATLAB) or list (Python) of per-attribute value matrices, so the single- and multi-attribute paths do not collide.

**Option Z normalisation.** `windowedSimilarity` normalises by the product of the *unwindowed* L2 norms of the two operands (rather than, say, the windowed norm of the context). This makes the profile value at each sweep position reflect both how well the local content matches the query and how much matching content is present. Multiplying either operand's weights by a constant leaves the profile unchanged.

**MaetDensity slot layout.** For a relative group, `buildExpTens` stores the effective-space Gaussian centres using a slot-0-anchored reduction: $v_i = u_{i+1} - u_0$ for $i = 1, \ldots, r-1$. This convention is inherited by all downstream consumers, including the cross-correlation shift lift in `windowedSimilarity`.

**Option II shape preservation.** All polymorphic dispatch paths preserve list shape: a length-1 list returns a length-1 cell / array, never a scalar. This matches NumPy and MATLAB cell-array semantics and makes downstream consumer code (e.g., a loop over the result) robust to single-element batches.

## [2.0.1] — 2026-04-15

### Fixed

- **`cosSimExpTens` / `cos_sim_exp_tens`** — Fixed incorrect transposition invariance in the periodic relative case (`isPer = true`, `isRel = true`). The quadratic form $Q$ now wraps pairwise differences between components of the wrapped difference vector, restoring exact transposition invariance on the circle. Previously, component-wise wrapping could introduce $\pm$period artefacts, producing cosine similarity discrepancies of $\sim 5 \times 10^{-7}$ for typical parameters ($\sigma = 10$, period = 1200). This issue was present in the original v1 implementation. Non-periodic and absolute results are unchanged.

### Changed

- **`batchCosSimExpTens` / `batch_cos_sim_exp_tens`** — Rewritten with three optimisations:
  - **Canonical-form deduplication** under `isPer` (mod reduction), `isRel` (transposition normalisation), and both (cyclic canonical form), reducing redundant computation when input rows contain equivalent pitch sets.
  - **Individual-set density caching** — `buildExpTens` is called once per unique multiset rather than once per unique pair.
  - **New `'precision'` parameter** — optionally rounds pitch and weight values to a specified number of decimal places before processing, ensuring nominally identical multisets differing only by floating-point noise are correctly deduplicated.
- User Guide updated to document the wrapped-pairwise-difference form of $Q$ for the periodic relative case.

## [2.0.0] — 2026-04-05

### Overview

Version 2.0.0 is a major rewrite of the Music Perception Toolbox. Analytical methods have replaced the previous numerical approximations wherever feasible (entropy computation, which has no known closed-form solution for Gaussian mixtures, remains discretized). The expectation tensor core has been restructured around precomputed density objects (`buildExpTens`), with fully vectorized computation and automatic memory-aware chunking. All existing functions have been given clearer names and a consistent interface, and a Python implementation has been added. The v1 dependency on the [Sparse Array Toolbox](https://github.com/andymilne/Sparse-Array-Toolbox) has been eliminated; v2 has no external dependencies.

Accessibility has been substantially improved: every function now includes a full help text with usage examples, a User Guide covers the conceptual foundations and provides a complete function reference for both languages, and nine demo scripts (in both MATLAB and Python) cover all major use cases.

The original `cosSimExpTens` function by David Bulger is preserved with full backward compatibility: the v1 nine-argument calling convention still works unchanged.

### Added

The following functions are entirely new in v2 (no v1 equivalent):

- **`buildExpTens`** — Precomputes an r-ad expectation tensor density object as a struct that can be reused across multiple calls to `evalExpTens` and `cosSimExpTens`.
- **`evalExpTens`** — Evaluates the expectation tensor density analytically at arbitrary query points with automatic memory-aware chunking and three normalisation modes. (Together, `buildExpTens` and `evalExpTens` replace `expectationTensor`, which built a discretized tensor on a grid.)
- **`batchCosSimExpTens`** — Batch cosine similarity of paired pitch multisets with automatic deduplication of repeated pairs, optional spectral enrichment, and progress reporting.
- **`estimateCompTime`** — Micro-benchmark-based computation time estimator, calibrated to the user's hardware.
- **`templateHarmonicity`** — Harmonicity via template cross-correlation (returns both hMax from Milne 2013 and hEntropy from Harrison & Pearce 2020).
- **`tensorHarmonicity`** — Harmonicity via expectation tensor lookup: evaluates the relative r-ad density of a harmonic series at the chord's intervals.
- **`virtualPitches`** — Virtual pitch (fundamental) salience profile via template cross-correlation.
- **`convertPitch`** — Converts between seven pitch/frequency scales (Hz, MIDI, cents, mel, Bark, ERB-rate, Greenwood cochlear position).
- **Python implementation** — A functionally identical Python package (`mpt`) using NumPy and SciPy. Python uses snake_case naming (e.g., `cos_sim_exp_tens`, `add_spectra`); a few names are shortened where the MATLAB suffix is redundant in a package namespace (e.g., `balanceCircular` → `balance`, `evennessCircular` → `evenness`). See the [User Guide](USER_GUIDE.md#4-api-conventions-matlab-vs-python) for the full mapping and calling convention differences.

### Changed

- **`cosSimExpTens`** — Now additionally accepts precomputed density structs from `buildExpTens` (preferred for repeated comparisons). The original nine-argument calling convention is fully backward compatible. David Bulger's analytical algorithm — which inspired much of the v2 architecture — has been further optimized at the implementation level: the double loop over all pairs of r-ad index sets has been replaced by fully vectorized array operations (3D implicit expansion and matrix multiplication), the explicit Riemannian metric matrix has been eliminated in favour of direct scalar arithmetic, and automatic memory-aware chunking has been added for large problems. Tuple enumeration and weight computation are factored into the precomputed density struct returned by `buildExpTens`, so these are computed once and reused across the three inner product calls (⟨x,x⟩, ⟨y,y⟩, ⟨x,y⟩) and across multiple comparisons. Added `'verbose'` flag for suppressing console output.

- Function names, signatures, and argument ordering have changed throughout. - The v1 dependency on the [Sparse Array Toolbox](https://github.com/andymilne/Sparse-Array-Toolbox) has been eliminated. v2 has no external dependencies.

- `nonLinDps`, `pDist` → not carried forward

## [1.0.0] — 2026-04-03 (retrospective tag)

The original Music Perception Toolbox as used in published work from 2011–2025. This version is permanently available as the `v1.0.0` tag and GitHub Release. It requires the [Sparse Array Toolbox](https://github.com/andymilne/Sparse-Array-Toolbox).

### Expectation tensor core

- `cosSimExpTens` — Cosine similarity of two r-ad expectation tensors. Originally by David Bulger (Macquarie University).
- `expectationTensor` — Discretized expectation tensor on a grid.
- `cosSim` / `expTensorSim` — Cosine similarity of precomputed tensors.
- `spectralize` — Harmonic spectral enrichment.

### Consonance and harmonicity

- `fSetRoughness` — Sensory roughness.
- `pSetSpectralEntropy` — Spectral entropy.
- `contextProbeSpecSim` — Context–probe spectral similarity.

### Balance and evenness (Fourier-based measures)

- `bal` — Balance.
- `eve` — Evenness.
- `dft2sss` / `pitch2Argand` — DFT of pitch-class sets.

### Scale and rhythm structure

- `coherence` — Coherence quotient.
- `sameness` — Sameness quotient.
- `stepEntropy` — Step-size entropy.
- `expTensorEntropy` / `rAdEntropy` — Expectation tensor entropy.
- `circApm` — Circular autocorrelation phase matrix.
- `edges` — Circular edge detection.
- `projCent` — Projected centroid.
- `modeHeight` — Mode height / mean offset.
- `markovS` — Optimal S-step Markov predictor. Originally by David Bulger.

### Other

- `gaussianKernel`, `histEntropy`, `tensorSum`, `circConv`, `pitch2Ind`, `ind2Pitch`, `peakPicker`, `noiseSignal`, `nonLinDps`, `pDist` — Helpers and utilities.
